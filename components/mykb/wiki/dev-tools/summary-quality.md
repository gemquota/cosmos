# Summary Quality

## Summary
A good Summary answers in a few sentences: what the concept is, where it sits in the system, and why it matters — readable on its own as a mini-article. It is the highest-value prose on the page: most readers and all link previews consume it first, so it must stand alone without the Details.

## Details
- Mechanism: the Summary is written last but read first — distill the article to its definition, context, and stakes; define jargon inline; make claims the Details support; end with a hook that motivates the rest of the page.
- Concrete example: a strong summary states the concept in one sentence, places it in the cluster (where it fits), and names the tradeoff (why it matters); a weak one repeats the title or opens with this article covers — which the reader already knows.
- Failure modes: summaries that are abstracts of history rather than definitions; jargon without definition, excluding newcomers; claims the Details never support (summary overpromises); summaries that require the Details to be understood, breaking link previews and skim readers; summaries that drift from the article after edits.
- Tradeoffs: a tight summary costs writing discipline but pays in discoverability and comprehension; the alternative, an unfocused opening, buries the point; the quality bar is that the summary survives alone — if it cannot, it is not done.
- Operational notes: review the summary at promotion time, keep it synchronized with the Details, and treat it as part of the content score.
- RSIS3 relevance: for mykb, summary quality is part of the content score and is reviewed at promotion time, not at stub time — the same review gate RSIS3 applies to its own artifacts.

## Practice
- Rewrite the summary from the finished Details: the act of distilling catches unsupported claims and missing context.
