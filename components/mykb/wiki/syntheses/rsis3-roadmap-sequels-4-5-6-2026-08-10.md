# RSIS3 roadmap sequels IV–VI (Phases 16–30)

Phases 1–15 are delivered. Three new sequel roadmaps extend the program to
30 phases; this note records the durable structure and the ordering rules
future sessions must respect.

## Maturity arcs (six-phase program)

- 1–5 Operational Autonomy · 6–10 Governed Intelligence · 11–15
  Distributed Autonomy · 16–20 Open Autonomy · 21–25 Ecosystem Autonomy ·
  26–30 Sovereign Autonomy.

## Ordering rules (durable constraints)

- Open Autonomy externalizes trust before adding capability: audit (16)
  precedes protocol (17); red-teaming (19) can only probe meaningfully
  once policy, budgets, invariants and authz exist; public API (20) reuses
  the Phase 12 identity chain — machine identities are users, not a
  parallel system.
- Ecosystem Autonomy is founded on identity (21) — signed federation,
  exchange value and swarm dispatch are meaningless until instances can
  authenticate each other. Exchange (22) and coordination (23) move
  knowledge and work with evidence (7), never anonymously. Local policy
  stays sovereign over foreign policy (24). Resilience (25) is the
  capstone: churn and partition are only testable after identity,
  exchange, dispatch and governance exist.
- Sovereign Autonomy internalizes the remaining human functions as
  monitored subsystems: meta-governance (26) precedes everything (policy
  revision must be evidence-driven before self-sustaining operation);
  sustainability (27) is the material prerequisite for an unbounded
  horizon; self-directed goals (28) need the self-model (10); stewardship
  (29) is generalization (11) applied to the population; endurance (30) is
  the proof of the whole program.
- The cross-roadmap invariant caps the program: **autonomy is cumulative
  but never unconditional** — Phase 30 enforces it as an executable
  meta-invariant (no adopted policy may silently relax a prior control).
- Every phase — in any roadmap — ends with a MyKB synthesis + snapshot
  regeneration per the standing L3 memory-consolidation practice.
