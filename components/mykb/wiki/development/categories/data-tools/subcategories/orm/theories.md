# Theories

## Summary

Theories in this cluster are the competing frameworks proposed to explain consciousness: integrated information, global workspace, predictive processing, and their philosophical rivals. Comparing them requires clear criteria for what counts as an explanation. It matters because the theory chosen shapes how cognitive claims about systems are evaluated. The page's role in the cluster is to keep the competing frameworks comparable.

## Details

- **Definition** — A theory of consciousness specifies the conditions under which a system has subjective experience and why.
- **Integrated information theory** — IIT proposes that consciousness corresponds to the system's integrated information, tying experience to causal structure.
- **Global workspace theory** — GWT explains conscious access as broadcasting information to a workspace that many specialized processes can read.
- **Predictive processing** — This family frames experience as the brain's best model of sensory causes, making perception an inference process.
- **Philosophical rivals** — Dualism, physicalism, and panpsychism differ on whether experience is fundamental, emergent, or reducible.
- **Evaluation criteria** — Strong theories predict empirical markers, explain dissociations, and survive adversarial cases rather than just describing them.
- **Failure modes** — Theories that are unfalsifiable, that redefine consciousness to fit the model, or that only address easy problems fail to explain experience.
- **Practical relevance** — The same criteria help the workspace evaluate claims about its own agentic systems' understanding and awareness.
- **Comparative table** — Laying theories side by side on scope, predictions, and empirical support makes differences legible.
- **Hardware analogies** — Engineered information systems often serve as test cases for whether theories explain more than brains.
- **Open problems** — Each theory leaves phenomena unexplained; naming those gaps is how the field advances.
