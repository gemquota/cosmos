## Overview

Philosophical Perspectives is an entity referenced once in the Cosmos session corpus inside the ORM data-tools category, with tags covering AST tooling, IDE work, ORM mapping, and single-page applications. The name suggests the session discussed the conceptual stances teams take toward object-relational mapping: whether the ORM should be treated as a transparent convenience, as a dangerous abstraction, or as one tool among several in a deliberate data strategy.

One familiar perspective holds that ORMs reduce boilerplate and keep domain code database-agnostic. Another holds that ORMs hide the real cost of generated SQL and encourage poor access patterns. A middle perspective treats the ORM as an optional layer: fine for CRUD and simple queries, but bypassed in favor of hand-written SQL for reports, bulk operations, and query-critical paths.

## Key Properties

- Productivity view: ORMs accelerate development by mapping rows to objects.
- Performance view: generated queries must be inspected and tuned like any other SQL.
- Pragmatic view: mix ORM and raw SQL deliberately rather than dogmatically.
- Architectural view: the choice shapes transaction, caching, and testing strategy.

## Summary

The durable takeaway is that ORM debates are usually about trade-offs, not absolutes: productivity against performance, abstraction against control, consistency against flexibility. Recording which perspective a team adopted and why keeps the decision auditable. The best systems state the policy explicitly — where the ORM is trusted, where it is bypassed, and what evidence drives that line.
