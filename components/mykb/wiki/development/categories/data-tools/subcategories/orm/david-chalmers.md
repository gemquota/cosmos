## The Philosopher

David Chalmers is an Australian philosopher of mind and cognitive scientist, best known for articulating the **hard problem of consciousness**: explaining why there is something it is like to undergo physical processing at all. His 1995 paper "Facing Up to the Problem of Consciousness" and his 1996 book *The Conscious Mind* distinguish easy problems — behavioral and functional questions such as attention, discrimination, and reportability — from the hard problem of subjective experience.

Key positions associated with Chalmers:

- **Naturalistic dualism** — consciousness is not reducible to physical facts, though it may supervene on them.
- **The zombie argument** — a conceivable physical duplicate lacking experience shows that physical facts do not entail phenomenal facts.
- **Panpsychism** — a later stance in which fundamental physical entities have experience-like properties.
- **Extended mind** — with Andy Clark, the claim that external artifacts can be part of the cognitive system.

His ideas surface in this knowledge base because sessions discussing agents, identity, and machine cognition borrow his vocabulary: "easy problems" maps to testable agent capabilities, while "hard problem" marks the explanatory gap that no benchmark currently closes.
