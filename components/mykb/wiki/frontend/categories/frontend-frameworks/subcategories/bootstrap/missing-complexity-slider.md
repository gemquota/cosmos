# Missing Complexity Slider

## Summary

Missing Complexity Slider is the bootstrap-cluster entity for the governance gap when tools offer no way to control their complexity: a missing dial between simple and advanced modes. Without a complexity control, products force every user through full complexity. It matters because complexity unmanaged becomes a tax on everyone. The concept names a gap; naming it is the first step to designing the control that fills it.

## Details

- **Definition** — The missing complexity slider names the absent affordance that would let users choose how much complexity a tool exposes.
- **Progressive disclosure** — Revealing advanced options on demand keeps default flows simple without hiding capability.
- **Audience layering** — Beginner, intermediate, and expert paths let each user operate at their comfort level.
- **Default choice** — The default complexity level is a product decision that shapes first impressions and churn.
- **Failure modes** — Settings buried in menus, or complexity that cannot be reduced at all, force users out.
- **Worked example** — An editor shows a minimal toolbar by default with an "advanced" toggle that reveals scripting and presets.
- **Practical relevance** — The pattern applies to configuration, features, and even documentation depth.
- **User journeys** — Distinct journeys for novices and experts each get the complexity they need.
- **Settings depth** — Advanced settings grouped behind a single toggle reduce surface without hiding capability.
- **Default safety** — Safe defaults mean users who never touch the slider still get a sound experience.
- **Design review** — Reviewing every new option against the question of who needs it keeps the complexity surface deliberate.
