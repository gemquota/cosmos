## Overview

AmbientLight is a concept or component related to lighting simulation or environmental effects, referenced in the Cosmos session corpus in visual and graphics contexts. The name comes from 3D rendering APIs, where an ambient light is a directionless source that raises the overall brightness of a scene so surfaces that receive no direct light are still visible. It is one of the classic lighting models alongside directional, point, and spot lights.

In real-time graphics, ambient light is usually a simplification: a constant term added to shading, optionally modulated by an ambient occlusion factor so crevices and corners stay dark. Physically based renderers replace flat ambient terms with image-based lighting, where the environment map provides the indirect illumination. The related topics — ajax, android, api, auth, aws, bash — describe the session mix around web and mobile rendering work rather than the lighting concept itself.

## Key Properties

- Role: ensures unlit surfaces are not rendered completely black.
- Simple form: a constant color added to the shaded result.
- Refinement: ambient occlusion and environment maps make the term plausible.
- Context: common in three.js, WebGL, and mobile graphics pipelines.
