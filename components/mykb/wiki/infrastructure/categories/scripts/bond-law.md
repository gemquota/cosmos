# Bond Law

## Summary

Bond Law is the scripts-cluster entity for the regularities governing chemical bonding: how atoms combine, what stabilizes bonds, and how structure follows from electron configuration. Bonding rules explain why molecules have the shapes and properties they do. They matter as a model of constraint-driven structure. Bonding laws are the local rules whose aggregate produces molecular structure, a pattern that recurs in engineered systems.

## Details

- **Definition** — Bond laws summarize the empirical and theoretical regularities that predict how and why atoms bond.
- **Octet rule** — Atoms tend to achieve stable electron configurations, usually eight valence electrons, which drives ion and molecule formation.
- **Bond types** — Ionic bonds transfer electrons; covalent bonds share them; metallic bonds delocalize them.
- **Bond order** — Single, double, and triple bonds differ in strength and length, with higher order meaning shorter, stronger bonds.
- **Geometry** — Electron-pair repulsion and hybridization convert bonding laws into predictable three-dimensional shapes.
- **Worked example** — Carbon's four valence electrons form four covalent bonds in a tetrahedral arrangement, the basis of organic structure.
- **Failure modes** — Treating rules like the octet rule as absolute, when many stable species violate them, misleads prediction.
- **Practical relevance** — Bonding models illustrate how simple local rules produce complex global structure, a theme shared with engineered systems.
- **Electronegativity** — The difference in electronegativity between atoms decides bond polarity, which drives solubility and reactivity.
- **Resonance** — Some molecules distribute bonding over multiple equivalent structures, stabilizing them beyond any single picture.
- **Prediction limits** — Quantum chemistry refines the classical laws; the simple rules remain the right first model.
- **Historical role** — Bonding laws emerged by organizing vast experimental data into simple rules, a model of how scientific knowledge becomes usable.
