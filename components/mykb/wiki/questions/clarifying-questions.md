# Clarifying Questions

## Summary

Clarifying questions are questions that pin down meaning, scope, and definitions before answering or acting. They resolve ambiguity in requests, instructions, and problems by making hidden assumptions explicit. Clarifying questions matter because a large share of poor answers and failed tasks trace back to misunderstood intent, and one good clarifying question can prevent hours of wrong-direction work.

## Details

- **Definition** — clarifying questions target ambiguity in terms, scope, constraints, and desired outcomes rather than asking for new information.
- **Types** — they address definitional ambiguity ("what do you mean by X?"), scope ("all users or just new ones?"), constraints ("within what budget?"), and intent ("what will this be used for?").
- **When to ask** — high stakes, irreversible actions, and obviously under-specified requests justify clarification; trivial friction does not.
- **Costs and benefits** — asking too many questions frustrates; asking none risks wasted work; the balance depends on context.
- **In agent systems** — clarification is a designed behavior: agents with a clarifying step reduce hallucinated assumptions and improve task success.
- **Worked example** — asked to "summarize the report", a good responder asks "for an executive or for the engineering team, and what length?", then delivers the right summary.
- **Failure modes** — leading questions that bias the answer, interrogating instead of clarifying, and ignoring the reply undermine the practice.
- **Practical relevance** — clarifying questions improve communication, requirements gathering, and prompt design for language models.
- **Relation to questioning techniques** — clarification is one function within the broader toolkit of probing, diagnostic, and open questions.
- **Prompt application** — instructing models to ask one clarifying question when a request is ambiguous often improves output relevance.
