# Mental Fatigue

## Summary

Mental fatigue is the state, arising from sustained cognitive effort, in which performance declines or the subjective cost of further effort rises. It matters because it degrades judgment, attention, and self-control precisely when work is hardest. Fatigue is both a measurable performance phenomenon and a motivational state, and its effects are influenced by breaks, sleep, and task design.

## Details

- **Definition** — Fatigue describes reduced readiness and capacity after prolonged demanding activity, distinct from boredom or low arousal alone.
- **Objective effects** — Sustained attention tasks show slowing, more errors, and rising variability as fatigue accumulates.
- **Subjective effects** — Perceived effort increases and the willingness to continue declines, even when raw ability remains.
- **Mechanisms** — Accounts invoke resource depletion, rising opportunity costs of effort, and increased distractibility; evidence supports a motivational component.
- **Worked example** — After hours of debugging, a developer makes simple copy-paste errors and rereads code repeatedly — classic objective fatigue signs.
- **Common failure modes** — Ignoring fatigue while forcing output, scheduling the hardest work at the end of long stretches, and mistaking fatigue for low ability.
- **Practical relevance** — Breaks, task rotation, sleep, and workload limits are practical interventions; pacing matters for both humans and long-running agents.
- **Limits** — Fatigue is task-specific and person-specific; laboratory measures correlate only modestly with real-world performance.
- **Sleep** — Sleep deprivation amplifies fatigue effects and impairs the metacognition needed to notice them, compounding risk.
- **Interventions** — Micro-breaks, task switching, and hydration have small but reliable effects; their main value is preventing the steepest decline.
- **Worked example** — A shift-schedule study finds error rates climb across the shift and reset after a rest period, supporting fatigue-aware scheduling.
- **Self-monitoring** — Because fatigue impairs self-assessment, objective checks — error rate, reaction time — beat subjective reports for deciding when to stop.
