# Bayesian Reasoning

## Summary

Bayesian Reasoning — Updating beliefs by combining prior probability with new evidence via Bayes' rule.

## Details

- Bayesian reasoning applies Bayes' theorem: posterior ∝ likelihood × prior. It is the normative standard for belief revision — new evidence reweights hypotheses in proportion to how well they predict the data — and it unifies perception, learning, and decision-making.
- Core concepts: priors (initial beliefs), likelihoods (evidence under each hypothesis), posteriors (revised beliefs), and prediction (marginalizing over hypotheses). Base-rate neglect is the classic failure: ignoring the prior and over-weighting vivid evidence.
- Worked example: a test is 99% sensitive and 95% specific, and 1% of people have the disease; a positive result yields roughly 17% posterior probability, not 99% — because the false-positive rate outweighs the base rate.
- Bayesian methods drive modern machine learning, cognitive science (the Bayesian brain), and forecasting; practical use demands transparent priors and calibration checks.
