# Debiasing Techniques

## Summary

Debiasing techniques are interventions that reduce the influence of cognitive biases on judgment and decision-making. They range from individual strategies — considering alternatives, seeking disconfirming evidence — to structural fixes that change the environment or process. They matter because most biases resist pure awareness, so effective debiasing usually changes incentives, defaults, or procedures rather than intentions.

## Details

- **Definition** — A debiasing technique targets a specific bias or family of biases by altering reasoning, information flow, or decision structure.
- **Individual strategies** — Consider-the-opposite, pre-mortems, and explicit base-rate reminders work for some biases but fade under pressure.
- **Structural fixes** — Checklists, decision rules, defaults, and blind evaluation remove discretion where bias operates, often outperforming willpower.
- **Statistical debiasing** — Regression to the mean corrections, weighting, and calibration training address judgment errors at the aggregate level.
- **Worked example** — A hiring panel adopts blind resume review and a scoring rubric, cutting the influence of anchoring and similarity heuristics on ratings.
- **Common failure modes** — Training that does not transfer, interventions that shift bias rather than remove it, and overconfidence that the bias is gone.
- **Practical relevance** — Teams and AI systems alike benefit from process-level debiasing, since individual vigilance is unreliable under load.
- **Limits** — Some biases are adaptive or unavoidable; debiasing should target high-cost decisions with known systematic error.
- **Evaluation** — Any debiasing program needs outcome measures; without before-and-after comparison, interventions feel effective and change nothing.
- **Context** — Techniques transfer poorly across domains; a fix that works for hiring may fail for medical diagnosis, so pilots and adaptation matter.
- **Worked example** — An investment committee adds a devil's advocate and a pre-mortem to every proposal; error tracking shows fewer missed risks over the following year.
- **Hygiene** — Debiasing is continuous, not one-time: refreshers, audits, and process review keep interventions from decaying into ceremony.
