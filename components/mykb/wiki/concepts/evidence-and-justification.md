# Evidence and Justification

## Summary

Evidence and Justification — What counts as evidence for a claim and when it justifies belief.

## Details

- Evidence is information that bears on the truth of a claim; justification is the standing evidence gives to belief. Internalist views require the believer to have access to reasons; externalist views (reliabilism) accept reliable processes even without access — the distinction drives how much trust we place in intuition versus measurement.
- Evidence varies in strength: direct observation, testimony, statistical data, and mechanism-based explanations each justify differently. Bayesian epistemology formalizes evidence as likelihood ratios that update probabilities.
- Worked example: a single eyewitness report justifies some belief, but a systematic review of randomized trials justifies far more; the same 'evidence' word hides very different justificatory weight.
- Modern issues: reproducibility, publication bias, and p-hacking degrade the evidential value of published claims — the replication crisis is an evidence-quality crisis.
