# Category Learning

## Summary

Category learning is the process by which people acquire the ability to group objects, events, or ideas into categories and apply those groupings to new instances. It matters because categories organize perception, memory, and decision-making, and their acquisition follows systematic, testable principles. Theories differ on whether categories are stored as prototypes, exemplars, or rules, with implications for how they are learned.

## Details

- **Definition** — Category learning is the acquisition of knowledge that supports classifying novel items and generalizing learned properties.
- **Theories** — Prototype theory stores averages, exemplar theory stores individual instances, and rule-based accounts store decision boundaries.
- **Feedback** — Supervised learning with corrective feedback, unsupervised clustering, and observational learning produce different category structures.
- **Worked example** — A radiologist learns to classify scans: early errors concentrate at boundary cases, and accuracy improves as feedback calibrates the decision boundary.
- **Common failure modes** — Overfitting to training exemplars, category boundaries that shift with misleading feedback, and transfer failure to novel contexts.
- **Practical relevance** — Instructional design, machine learning, and agent knowledge organization all draw on these principles.
- **Variants** — Artificial category learning experiments isolate the mechanisms; natural categories carry real-world noise and competing features.
- **Limits** — No single theory explains all learning; mixed models that combine rules, prototypes, and exemplars fit the data best.
- **Category structure** — Family resemblance categories with many overlapping features are learned differently from rule-defined categories with clean boundaries.
- **Order effects** — The sequence of training examples matters: interleaving categories beats blocked practice for learning boundaries.
- **Worked example** — A classifier's training curriculum interleaves examples across classes after showing prototypes first, improving boundary accuracy over random ordering.
- **Applications** — The same principles inform teaching, UX categorization, and the design of few-shot demonstrations for models.
