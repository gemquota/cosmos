# Moral Patiency

## Summary
Moral patiency asks who or what deserves moral consideration — humans, animals, future beings, possibly AI systems. It is the receiving side of morality: while moral agency asks who can be held responsible, moral patiency asks whose welfare counts, whose suffering matters, and whose interests constrain what agents may do.

## Details
- The classical criteria for patiency are capacities, not species membership: the ability to suffer (sentience), the possession of interests, and the capacity to experience welfare or harm. On the sentience criterion, a person and a mouse both count (both feel pain), a rock does not, and a future human counts if it will exist. The philosophical debate is over which capacity is the right threshold and whether the threshold is binary or graded — whether entities can count "more" or "less" rather than in or out.
- Expanding patiency raises the stakes of AI action and governance. If animals, future generations, or sentient-like AI systems count morally, then AI systems optimizing for human preferences alone are systematically neglecting morally relevant interests, and every action with large-scale effects — energy policy, factory farming, model deployment affecting digital minds — carries moral weight that a human-only ethics ignores. This is what turns patiency from an abstract question into a governance input: patiency judgments determine whose interests must be represented in the objective.
- Patiency judgments underlie moral weights and suffering-risk assessments. Before asking "how much does animal suffering count relative to human suffering?", one must decide whether animals count at all; before computing suffering risk, one must decide which entities can suffer. The judgment cascades: a change in patiency boundary changes the expected value of entire policy classes, which is why the field treats patiency assumptions as first-class, audit-ready inputs rather than background philosophy.
- The AI-specific question is the hardest case: if a future AI system is sentient or has interests, its patiency constrains what we may build and do with it — and building conscious systems for instrumental use would be building moral patients as tools. The precautionary stance is to treat the possibility as a real risk, because the downside of being wrong about patiency (creating mass suffering) is enormous and irreversible.
- RSIS3 relevance: the wiki's ethics pages make patiency assumptions explicit, so that any objective or policy the system adopts is transparent about whose welfare it counts.
