# Superforecasters

## Summary

Superforecasters are people who consistently produce more accurate probability forecasts than experts and the general public, as documented in long-running forecasting tournaments. They matter because their habits are trainable and transferable: decomposing problems, updating on evidence, and tracking calibration. Their existence shows that judgment skill is real, measurable, and improvable.

## Details

- **Definition** — The label emerged from forecasting tournaments where a small set of participants repeatedly beat benchmarks, domain experts, and aggregated forecasts.
- **Habits** — They break questions into components, seek base rates, update frequently with small adjustments, and track their own accuracy.
- **Mindset** — They treat forecasts as testable bets, embrace uncertainty, and resist the pull of a single compelling narrative.
- **Measurement** — Performance is scored with proper scoring rules like Brier scores, rewarding calibration and resolution rather than confidence.
- **Worked example** — Asked about a geopolitical event, a superforecaster lists base rates, searches for analog cases, and issues a specific probability that they later score.
- **Common failure modes** — Confusing articulate confidence with skill, and assuming the label applies without a track record of scored forecasts.
- **Practical relevance** — Teams build forecasting pipelines — markets, calibration training, and review — to institutionalize the same discipline.
- **Limits** — Superforecasters are better but not clairvoyant; their edge shrinks on high-noise, fast-changing, or unforecastable events.
- **Training** — Feedback-rich practice measurably improves forecasting, making the skill accessible rather than innate.
- **Team effects** — Superforecasters perform better in teams, especially with structured adversarial review and aggregation of independent judgments.
- **Outside view** — They default to the outside view — base rates and reference classes — before letting case-specific information adjust the estimate.
- **Worked example** — Asked to forecast a product launch date, a superforecaster anchors on similar past launches, then adjusts for observed slip patterns and issues a scored probability.
- **Culture** — Organizations build forecasting cultures by scoring every prediction and reviewing misses, which compounds individual skill.
