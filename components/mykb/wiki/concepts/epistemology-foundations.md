# Epistemology Foundations

## Summary

Epistemology Foundations — The philosophical study of knowledge: its nature, sources, and limits.

## Details

- Epistemology asks what knowledge is, what can be known, and how belief becomes justified. The classical analysis treats knowledge as justified true belief; Gettier cases show justification plus truth can still fall short, spurring refinements (reliabilism, virtue epistemology, and externalist accounts).
- Core questions: the sources of knowledge (perception, introspection, reason, testimony, memory), the structure of justification (foundationalism versus coherentism), and the limits of knowledge (skepticism, uncertainty).
- Worked example: you believe the train leaves at 9:00 because the schedule says so, and it does — justified true belief; but if the schedule was wrong and the train merely happened to leave then, the same belief fails as knowledge under Gettier-style analysis.
- Applied epistemology now studies testimony and experts, misinformation, and AI-generated claims — exactly the concerns of a knowledge system with provenance.
- mykb relevance: provenance, sourcing, and status fields are the wiki's epistemology layer — they encode how a claim came to be believed.
