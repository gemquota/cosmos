# Metaphor Comprehension

## Summary

Metaphor comprehension is the process of understanding one domain in terms of another — mapping structure from a familiar source onto a target. It matters because metaphor is not decoration but a basic mechanism of thought and language, shaping how concepts are framed and communicated. Comprehension involves retrieving the source, aligning correspondences, and selecting the relevant aspects.

## Details

- **Definition** — A metaphor asserts that one thing is another, inviting the listener to map selected structure from the source domain onto the target.
- **Mapping** — Comprehension aligns correspondences — as in argument is war, where attack, defense, and victory map from conflict onto debate.
- **Speed and automaticity** — Familiar metaphors are understood as quickly as literal language; novelty and complexity slow processing.
- **Mechanisms** — Accounts differ: direct access of the metaphor's meaning, comparison of source and target, or conceptual integration through blending.
- **Context** — Comprehension depends on context and conventionality; a metaphor that works in one discourse fails in another.
- **Worked example** — Reading time is money, a reader maps spend, save, and waste from finance onto time use, and reasons about scheduling accordingly.
- **Common failure modes** — Literalizing metaphors, mapping unwanted structure, and cross-cultural mismatches where the source domain is unfamiliar.
- **Practical relevance** — Explanation, persuasion, and product framing all lean on metaphor, so choosing the source domain shapes what audiences infer.
- **Variants** — Novel versus conventional metaphors, verbal versus visual metaphors, and similes differ in processing and force.
- **Limits** — Metaphor is flexible but not unbounded; comprehension is constrained by what the source domain actually affords.
- **Development** — Metaphor comprehension develops with language and experience; children first understand concrete mappings and only later master abstract ones.
- **Brain basis** — Neuroimaging shows metaphor processing recruits semantic and sometimes sensorimotor regions, supporting embodied accounts of comprehension.
- **Worked example** — In a negotiation, the metaphor of chess frames moves as strategic plays, biasing participants toward calculated rather than cooperative responses.
- **Measurement** — Reading-time and priming experiments measure comprehension, distinguishing effortless access from effortful comparison.
