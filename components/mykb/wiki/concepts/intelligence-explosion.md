# Intelligence Explosion

## Summary
The intelligence explosion is the hypothesis that an AI able to improve its own intelligence will do so at an accelerating rate, quickly surpassing human capability. I. J. Good named the dynamic in 1965; modern debate focuses on whether and how fast such a takeoff could occur.

## Details
- **Key premise** — intelligence is, at least in part, an input to further intelligence, so improvements compound.
- **Takeoff variants** — slow, fast, and moderate scenarios differ in how much warning and control society has.
- **Crucial consideration** — even without an explosion, compounding self-improvement changes the safety calculus because failures become harder to reverse.
- **Evidence debate** — LLM scaling so far shows steady, not explosive, gains; emergent abilities appear when metrics change, per the 2022 mirage critique.
- **RSIS3 framing** — the nine-loop stack implements a bounded, human-paced improvement loop: deliberate recursion with checkpoints rather than runaway optimization.
