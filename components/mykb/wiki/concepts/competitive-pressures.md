# Competitive Pressures

## Summary
Competitive pressures arise when multiple actors race to deploy AI capability first, creating incentives to cut corners on safety, transparency, and verification. The AI arms race framing treats this as the central governance problem: even well-intentioned labs may be forced by rivals into risky speed.

## Details
- **Mechanism** — first-mover advantage, funding races, and national security stakes reward speed; safety work is visible only in failure.
- **Multipolar dynamics** — with several roughly equal actors, no one can unilaterally pause without losing the race.
- **Evidence** — capability-forecasting and deployment timelines influence funding and policy; responsible-scaling policies are the counterweight.
- **Mitigations** — coordinated norms, compute governance, pre-commitments, and audit requirements.
- **RSIS3 relevance** — the bundle is explicitly a slow, deliberate improvement loop; its practices document frames speed as a risk, not a goal.
