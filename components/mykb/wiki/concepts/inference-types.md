# Inference Types

## Summary

Inference Types — Forms of reasoning: deduction, induction, and abduction.

## Details

- Inference is deriving conclusions from premises. Three classic types: deduction (guaranteed truth if premises hold), induction (generalization from samples, probable not certain), and abduction (inference to the best explanation). Each has distinct standards of validity and failure modes.
- Deduction is truth-preserving but content-free; induction scales knowledge but can be fooled by biased samples; abduction generates hypotheses but can overfit to plausible stories. Modern probabilistic inference unifies all three under belief updating.
- Worked example: all F is G, F is x → x is G (deduction); all observed swans are white → all swans are white (induction); the ground is wet and it rained → it rained (abduction, best explanation).
- Reasoning skill requires matching the type to the goal: proof needs deduction, prediction needs induction, diagnosis needs abduction. Cognitive research shows people systematically misuse the types — belief bias and confirmation seeking are type confusions.
