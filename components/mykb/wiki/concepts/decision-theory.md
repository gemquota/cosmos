# Decision Theory

## Summary

Decision Theory — Formal study of choosing among alternatives under uncertainty.

## Details

- Decision theory models choice: given options, uncertain states, and preferences, which action should a rational agent take? The prescriptive core is expected utility — choose the act maximizing probability-weighted value — with descriptive research showing people systematically deviate from it.
- Components: outcomes and utilities (what you value), probabilities (beliefs about states), and decision rules (maximize expected utility, or lexicographic, satisficing, minimax variants). Subjective expected utility allows personal probabilities, connecting decision theory to Bayesian reasoning.
- Worked example: choosing whether to study a new framework: value × probability of benefit for 'learn now' versus 'wait' — the option with higher expected utility wins, but only if the probabilities are calibrated.
- Behavioral economics documents violations: loss aversion, ambiguity aversion, framing, and probability weighting. Decision quality practice (journals, red teams) applies normative theory while respecting human limits.
