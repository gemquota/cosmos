# Seed Article Criteria

## Summary
Seed article criteria decide which new topics deserve a first article rather than just a capture in the inbox or a raw note. They are the entry filter of the knowledge base: a wiki that writes an article for every passing thought drowns in unmaintained stubs, while one that never writes new articles stagnates. The criteria make the "should this become a page?" decision checkable.

## Details
- Typical tests: the topic is stable enough to write about, it recurs across sessions, it connects to existing articles, and a source exists to ground it. Stability filters out ephemera — a tool that will be obsolete next month, a trend that will pass — because a wiki page is a commitment to maintain content. Recurrence filters out one-offs — if the topic never comes up again, no future reader needs the page. Connectivity filters out islands — a topic with no existing articles to link to and from will be an orphan on arrival. Source grounding filters out speculation — a page needs something citable so its claims are verifiable, not vibes.
- A seed that fails the tests is parked, not written — premature articles become stubs that nobody promotes. This is the operational heart of the criteria: the alternative to "no article yet" is not "bad article now" but "capture in the inbox, revisit when the criteria are met". Parking costs nothing and preserves the option; writing prematurely creates a stub-ratio liability that someone must later promote or delete. The criteria are therefore a gate that protects the promotion pipeline as much as the wiki's quality.
- The failure modes run both ways. Over-strict criteria starve the wiki — topics never reach the threshold because no one captures them long enough to test recurrence. Over-permissive criteria flood it — the inbox becomes the wiki, and the stub-ratio metric climbs until nobody can tell signal from noise. The calibration question — how much recurrence, how strong a source — is the curation judgment the criteria make visible and debatable.
- For mykb, seeding is the entry point of the acquisition workflow, and the seed list feeds growth-ratio by design: the criteria determine what enters the pipeline, so they control the numerator of the growth metric, and relaxing or tightening them is a policy decision about how fast the wiki should grow.
