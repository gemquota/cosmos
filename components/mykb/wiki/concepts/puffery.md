# Puffery

## Summary

Puffery is exaggerated or subjective praise — claims like best, revolutionary, or unmatched — that inflates a subject without verifiable specifics. It matters because puffery erodes trust, misleads readers, and is often legally tolerated while being ethically dubious. In technical and encyclopedic writing, puffery is a quality defect to be stripped in favor of verifiable claims.

## Details

- **Definition** — Puffery consists of subjective superlatives and vague enthusiasm that cannot be confirmed or falsified.
- **Where it appears** — Marketing copy, product pages, resumes, and even documentation drift into puffery when writers substitute enthusiasm for evidence.
- **Why it persists** — Puffery is cheap, sounds positive, and is legally hard to challenge, so it survives where accountability is weak.
- **Worked example** — Calling a tool the most powerful solution on the market is puffery; stating that it processes ten thousand requests per second is a checkable claim.
- **Detection** — Words like best, leading, unique, and world-class, without comparators or data, are reliable markers of puffery.
- **Common failure modes** — Letting marketing voice leak into technical docs, describing one's own work with superlatives, and reviewing copy with the same standards as claims.
- **Practical relevance** — Encyclopedic and knowledge-base writing requires neutral tone: claims need sources, comparisons need baselines.
- **Remedy** — Rewrite puffery as specific, sourced assertions; where evidence is missing, say so instead of inflating.
- **Limits** — Not all positive description is puffery; the line is drawn by verifiability and the burden of comparison.
- **Legal note** — Advertising law tolerates puffery as non-actionable opinion, which is why marketers use it; the same tolerance does not extend to factual claims.
- **Review practice** — A neutral review pass that flags superlatives and asks for evidence converts puffery into either specific claims or removals.
- **Worked example** — A draft article says the framework is the fastest; review rewrites it as the framework completing the benchmark suite in the shortest measured time, with the benchmark named.
- **Tone** — Encyclopedic tone prefers verbs and data over adjectives, making verifiability the test of every assertion.
