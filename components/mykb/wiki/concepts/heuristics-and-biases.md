# Heuristics and Biases

## Summary

Heuristics and Biases — Fast mental shortcuts and the systematic judgment errors they produce.

## Details

- Heuristics are quick, frugal rules that usually work and sometimes fail systematically. Tversky and Kahneman's research program catalogued the failures: availability (ease of recall), representativeness (similarity ignoring base rates), anchoring (insufficient adjustment), and affect-driven judgment.
- These biases are not mere noise — they are predictable, measurable, and consequential in finance, medicine, law, and forecasting. Later work (Gigerenzer) argues many heuristics are ecologically rational in natural environments; both camps agree that context decides whether a shortcut helps or harms.
- Worked example: after a plane crash dominates the news, availability inflates perceived flight risk; the statistics — safest mode of travel — are neglected because the images are easier to retrieve.
- Mitigation: base rates, consider-the-opposite, structured checklists, and calibration feedback. Awareness alone rarely fixes bias; process changes do.
