# Story Grammar

## Summary

Story grammar describes the conventional structure of narratives — settings, characters, initiating events, attempts, and outcomes — that organizes how stories are told and understood. It matters because structured narratives are remembered better and comprehended faster than unstructured sequences. Story grammar is both a descriptive framework for narratives and a cognitive schema for comprehension.

## Details

- **Definition** — Story grammar specifies the parts of a story and their canonical order: setting, initiating event, internal response, attempt, consequence, and reaction.
- **Comprehension** — Readers use story structure to anticipate what comes next and to integrate events into a coherent model.
- **Memory** — Well-structured stories are recalled better, and recall tends to preserve grammar order even when details are lost.
- **Development** — Children acquire story grammar gradually; explicit instruction in story structure improves comprehension and writing.
- **Worked example** — A summary of a fable retains the initiating event and consequence while omitting description, matching the grammar's central slots.
- **Common failure modes** — Over-applying Western narrative conventions to other traditions, and treating grammar as a formula that guarantees quality.
- **Practical relevance** — Documentation, explanations, and agent-generated reports benefit from narrative structure that matches reader expectations.
- **Variants** — Episodic and thematic structures differ from canonical grammar; genre modifies which elements matter.
- **Cross-cultural scope** — Narrative conventions differ across cultures and genres, so grammar is a toolkit of common patterns rather than a universal template.
- **Instruction** — Teaching story grammar improves both comprehension questions and written narratives, especially for developing readers.
- **Worked example** — A study of recall shows participants reconstruct a scrambled story into canonical order, evidencing the grammar's organizing role.
- **Generation** — Automated storytelling and report generation can use story grammar to order content the way readers expect.
