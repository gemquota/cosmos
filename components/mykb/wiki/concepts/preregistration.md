# Preregistration

## Summary

Preregistration is the practice of recording hypotheses, methods, and analysis plans publicly before collecting data. It matters because it separates confirmatory from exploratory analysis, reducing p-hacking and hindsight storytelling. A preregistration turns the planned analysis into a commitment that later results can be checked against.

## Details

- **Definition** — A preregistration is a timestamped document stating the research questions, planned measures, sample, and statistical analyses before outcomes are known.
- **Confirmatory vs exploratory** — Pre-planned analyses test hypotheses; post-hoc findings are labeled exploratory, so readers can weigh evidence accordingly.
- **Anti-p-hacking** — Committing to analysis choices in advance prevents data-dependent decisions such as dropping outliers or switching tests until significance appears.
- **Flexibility** — Preregistration does not forbid changes; it requires documenting deviations, which preserves honesty while allowing learning.
- **Worked example** — A team preregisters a comparison of two interventions with a sample size and primary endpoint, then reports both the planned test and any deviations.
- **Common failure modes** — Vague preregistrations that leave wiggle room, registering after results are known, and treating exploratory results as confirmatory.
- **Practical relevance** — Preregistration is a cornerstone of open science, directly targeting the incentives behind the replication crisis.
- **Variants** — Analysis plans, registered reports with peer review before data collection, and trial registries are progressively stronger commitments.
- **Limits** — It cannot fix poor measurement or flawed theory; it disciplines the analysis, not the underlying design.
- **Registry timing** — The timestamp proves the plan preceded the data; even private registries with embargoes establish the sequence of events.
- **Levels** — Pre-analysis plans inside a paper, public registrations, and registered reports differ in how binding and how visible the commitment is.
- **Worked example** — A clinical trial preregisters the primary outcome and analysis; when the data arrive, the team runs the planned test and separately reports exploratory findings.
- **Critique** — Preregistration can be gamed with vague or overloaded plans, which is why specific, falsifiable commitments matter more than the act itself.
