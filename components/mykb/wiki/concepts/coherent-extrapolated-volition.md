# Coherent Extrapolated Volition

## Summary
Coherent extrapolated volition (CEV) is the hypothetical outcome of humans becoming more informed, more intelligent, and more coherent about what they want. Proposed by Eliezer Yudkowsky, it is an alignment target for superintelligence: instead of encoding today's imperfect preferences, a system should act in humanity's extrapolated interest — what we would want if we knew more and thought more carefully.

## Details
- The motivation is the standard preference-updating problem: naive value encoding bakes in present ignorance, bias, and inconsistency. CEV answers "what should the AI optimize?" with "the fixed point of human reflection" — the values we would converge on after unlimited information, deliberation, and mutual understanding. The AI's job is to help us become what we would endorse being, and to do that without overriding us.
- The proposal has three components: extrapolation (imagine humanity knowing everything relevant and reasoning clearly), coherence (resolve contradictions so values form a consistent whole rather than a bundle of conflicting impulses), and volition (the result is a matter of human will, not an external moral truth). This makes CEV explicitly anti-paternalistic in intent: it wants what we would want, not what an optimizer thinks is good for us.
- CEV is philosophically rich and operationally under-specified. Defining the extrapolation process is itself a value-laden choice (whose deliberation counts? how much weight to coherence over current preference?), no algorithm computes the fixed point, and there is a deep circularity: a superintelligence strong enough to extrapolate us correctly is the very thing whose alignment we are trying to specify. Practical proposals therefore treat CEV as a direction-setting ideal rather than an implementable objective, with more tractable intermediates like corrigibility and iterative value negotiation taking its place on the operational agenda.
- Failure modes: CEV can be used to launder the designer's own values ("I extrapolate to what I would want"), it presumes a single coherent convergence that may not exist, and it creates a massive inference burden — getting it wrong at superintelligent scale is itself a catastrophe.
- RSIS3 relevance: the bundle's values are explicit practices, a local and concrete version of CEV. Where CEV is the hypothetical ideal, mykb's documented practices are an actual, inspectable value record — and the system's improvement loops are the extrapolation process in miniature.
