# Bounded Rationality

## Summary
Bounded rationality is the study of decisions made with limited time, information, and compute — the real condition of every agent. It matters because optimality is usually unaffordable; the goal becomes good-enough under constraints. Heuristics are the result, not the failure.

## Details
- Herbert Simon's original framing: satisficing instead of optimizing.
- Heuristics are fast, frugal, and sometimes systematically biased.
- Agent design: budgets on search, retries, and context are bounds on rationality.
- Open questions: principled resource allocation across reasoning methods.
