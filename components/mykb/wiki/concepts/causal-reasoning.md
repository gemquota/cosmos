# Causal Reasoning

## Summary

Causal Reasoning — Inferring and using cause-effect relations to explain, predict, and intervene.

## Details

- Causal reasoning identifies and exploits cause-effect relations: it explains outcomes, predicts under interventions, and guides action. Philosophical accounts range from regularity and counterfactual dependence to mechanisms; Pearl's causal framework formalizes it with graphs, interventions, and counterfactuals.
- Key concepts: correlation is not causation; confounders create spurious associations; randomized experiments identify causes; do-calculus and structural models extend identification to observational data. Humans learn causes from contingency, mechanism, and verbal explanation, but also suffer systematic errors (illusion of control, omitted-variable blindness).
- Worked example: observing that ice cream sales and drownings rise together does not imply ice cream causes drowning — the confound is hot weather; a causal graph plus an intervention (ban ice cream, watch drownings) disambiguates.
- Counterfactual reasoning ('what would have happened if not X') is the core of causal judgment and of blame and credit assignment.
- mykb relevance: separating correlation from causation in telemetry is the same discipline applied to agent outcomes.
