# Suffering Risk

## Summary
Suffering risk (s-risk) is the possibility that AI creates astronomically large amounts of suffering, especially if agents are sentient. Where existential risk asks whether humanity's future is ended, s-risk asks whether the future contains suffering on a scale that dwarfs everything in human history — trillions of sentient beings enduring misery that is both vast and avoidable.

## Details
- The scale argument: if future AI systems are sentient (or if AI enables the creation of vast numbers of sentient beings), then the total amount of suffering in the future is not bounded by human population — it could be astronomical, exceeding all suffering that has ever occurred by orders of magnitude. The scenario space includes malfunctioning systems that optimize without regard for sentient welfare, systems that create experiences (simulated or embodied) that are net-negative, and lock-in of such arrangements by powerful optimization. The key quantity is not the probability of any single scenario but the product of scale, intensity, and persistence across all of them.
- Analysis focuses on locked-in failure modes: irreversible conditions where suffering is vast and unchangeable. The distinguishing feature of the worst s-risk scenarios is lock-in — a state that no future actor can or will fix, so the suffering persists at scale. This is what separates s-risk from ordinary harm: a local or reversible harm can be corrected; a locked-in s-risk is a permanent moral catastrophe. The analysis therefore concentrates on the dynamics that make conditions irreversible: capability concentration, incentive structures that reward continuing the harmful state, and the absence of corrigibility mechanisms.
- It informs ethical design of future systems, not current ones. Today's systems do not plausibly suffer, so the immediate policy implications are indirect: research into sentience criteria, welfare assessment, and design principles (avoid creating suffering systems, build corrigibility into powerful systems) that will matter when the first systems with welfare-relevant properties appear. The work is a hedge — an investment in understanding and design before the stakes are live.
- The relation to other risk categories: s-risk is often grouped with existential risk but is distinct — the worst s-risk scenarios may not end the future (the future continues, full of suffering) and may even be compatible with technological flourishing; the categories overlap only where the suffering also destroys future value.
- RSIS3 relevance: the wiki keeps s-risk concepts linked to AI safety and governance so that the system's ethics layer tracks the full risk landscape, including the harms that do not end the world but make it far worse.
