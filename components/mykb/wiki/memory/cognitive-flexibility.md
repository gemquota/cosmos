# Cognitive Flexibility

## Summary

Cognitive Flexibility — Capacity to shift between perspectives, tasks, or strategies.

## Details

- Cognitive flexibility is the executive ability to switch attention, perspectives, or strategies when conditions change — the 'shift' component alongside working-memory updating and inhibition. It is measured with task switching, set-shifting (Wisconsin Card Sort), and reversal-learning paradigms.
- Flexibility supports learning: interleaved practice trains discrimination and shifting; rigid learners persist with obsolete strategies. It declines with stress, fatigue, and age, and improves with training and varied practice.
- Worked example: a developer who can hold two mental models of a system — the legacy architecture and the refactor target — and switch between them during migration is exercising cognitive flexibility; getting stuck in one frame causes integration errors.
- Neural basis: frontoparietal control networks and the basal ganglia orchestrate switching; dopamine modulates the trade-off between stability and flexibility.
