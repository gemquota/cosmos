# Mnemonics Research

## Summary
Mnemonics research is the evidence base for memory aids that elaborate, organize, or peg information: methods like the keyword method, method of loci, peg words, and chunking. The consistent finding across decades of studies is that mnemonic techniques substantially improve recall of serial and list material, with the gains strongest for arbitrary associations and weakest for conceptual understanding.

## Details
- **Evidence base** — mnemonic techniques improve recall by adding structure, imagery, or meaning to otherwise arbitrary material. The keyword method and method of loci show robust gains in paired-associate learning studies: learners using imagery-based strategies routinely outperform rote rehearsal on immediate and delayed tests.
- **Boundaries** — benefits are strongest for serial or list material; benefits for conceptual understanding are weaker. A mnemonic can encode the order of the OSI layers or the spelling of a word, but it does not teach what a protocol does — comprehension still requires genuine elaboration and practice.
- **Mechanism framing** — modern research frames mnemonics as a form of elaborative encoding and dual coding: the to-be-learned item is enriched with additional cues (images, locations, verbal links) that provide multiple independent retrieval paths, and retrieval practice on those paths is what strengthens the trace.
- **Concrete example** — a study comparing keyword-method vocabulary learning (linking a foreign word to a similar-sounding English keyword with an interactive image) against rote repetition finds recall advantages of 20–50 percentage points on immediate tests, with the advantage persisting across delays when the images are rehearsed.
- **Failure modes** — over-reliance on the mnemonic without retrieval practice, so the image is remembered but the target fact is not; poorly constructed keywords that add confusion instead of cues; and applying mnemonics to material that is already meaningful, where the extra encoding layer is wasted effort or even interference.
- **Tradeoffs** — mnemonics are cheap to apply and high-yield for the right material, but they are not a general learning strategy: they do not improve comprehension, transfer, or problem-solving, and they add an encoding step that slows initial learning. Pair them with spaced repetition so the mnemonic path is actually rehearsed.
- **RSIS3/mykb relevance** — this evidence base matters to the memory layer because it sets expectations for what memory aids can do: the wiki's structured, linked notes are the comprehension layer, and mnemonics are a complementary tool for the arbitrary facts that still need to be recalled verbatim.
