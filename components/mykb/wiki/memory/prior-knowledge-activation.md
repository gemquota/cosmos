# Prior Knowledge Activation

## Summary

Prior Knowledge Activation — Bringing existing knowledge to bear before and during new learning.

## Details

- Prior knowledge is the strongest single predictor of new learning: what you already know determines what you can encode, chunk, and connect. Activation — deliberately retrieving and structuring relevant prior knowledge before study — improves comprehension, elaboration, and retention.
- Mechanisms: activated knowledge provides schema slots for new facts, enables elaborative interrogation, and guides attention to what matters. But prior knowledge cuts both ways: wrong or biased priors can distort encoding and resist correction.
- Worked example: before reading about the spacing effect, a learner recalls what they know about forgetting curves; the new material then attaches to existing structure, and recall is better than for a learner who read cold.
- Instructional moves: advance organizers, pre-questions, K-W-L charts, and misconception elicitation. Activating misconceptions, however, requires confrontation and replacement, not just repetition.
