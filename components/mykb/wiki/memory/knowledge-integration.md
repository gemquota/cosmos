# Knowledge Integration

## Summary

Knowledge Integration — Weaving separate ideas into coherent, connected understanding.

## Details

- Knowledge integration is the process of linking separate facts and ideas into a coherent web — connecting new information to prior knowledge, resolving conflicts between sources, and organizing parts under principles. Linn's knowledge-integration framework studies how learners' fragmented ideas coalesce with instruction.
- Mechanisms: eliciting existing ideas, adding new normative ideas, and prompting comparison and reconciliation. Integration predicts transfer: integrated knowledge is flexible, while fragmented facts stay inert.
- Worked example: a learner holds separate ideas — 'spacing helps memory' and 'testing helps memory' — until an integrative explanation ('both create effortful retrieval that strengthens traces') fuses them into one principle that transfers to new study tasks.
- Barriers: compartmentalization, conflicting sources, and shallow links. Concept maps, cross-links, and synthesis notes are integration tools.
- mykb relevance: the wiki's Related blocks and syntheses are integration machinery — knowledge that links is knowledge that transfers.
