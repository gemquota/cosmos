# Sleep and Memory

## Summary

Sleep and Memory — How sleep consolidates, reorganizes, and protects memories.

## Details

- Sleep is a privileged state for memory: during slow-wave sleep, hippocampal traces are replayed and reorganized into neocortical storage; REM sleep supports emotional and procedural consolidation. Sleep deprivation impairs encoding, consolidation, and retrieval the next day.
- Key findings: memories reactivated during sleep (cued by sounds or odors) improve; naps consolidate declarative and motor learning; sleep after learning reduces interference and strengthens gist extraction. The systems-consolidation account places sleep at the center of the transfer from hippocampus to cortex.
- Worked example: studying vocabulary in the evening and sleeping beats the same study followed by daytime activity — the night's replay integrates the words into long-term memory and shields them from interference.
- Practical guidance: prioritize sleep before and after learning, use spaced study plus sleep rather than all-nighters, and treat sleep hygiene as part of the study system.
- mykb relevance: memory consolidation and spaced repetition entries assume the learner sleeps between reviews — the scheduler's gaps are sleep windows.
