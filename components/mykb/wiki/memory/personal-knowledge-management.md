# Personal Knowledge Management

## Summary
Personal knowledge management (PKM) is the deliberate system a person uses to capture, connect, and retrieve what they learn. It spans tools, methods, and habits — from paper index cards to AI-assisted wikis like mykb — and its core loop is capture, organize, retrieve, create, repeated until the system becomes part of how the person thinks.

## Details
- **Loop** — capture → organize → retrieve → create; tools serve whichever step is weakest. Most PKM failures are loop failures: capture that never gets organized, organization that never gets retrieved, or retrieval that never feeds creation.
- **Methods** — Zettelkasten, PARA, progressive summarization, and evergreen notes are the common playbooks. They are not competitors but answers to different questions: Zettelkasten answers "how do I make notes reusable?", PARA answers "where does each note live?", progressive summarization answers "how do I keep notes scannable?", and evergreens answer "what should a mature note look like?".
- **Concrete example** — a professional reads twenty articles a month, captures one atomic note per useful claim, links each to related concepts, and runs a weekly review that promotes the strongest notes to evergreens and deletes the weakest; over a year the collection compounds into a reference that answers questions faster than a fresh search.
- **Failure modes** — tool-hopping, where the system is rebuilt every few months and the knowledge never accumulates; capture-only behavior, where nothing is ever organized or retrieved; and the perfectionist trap, where notes must be perfect before they are useful, so almost nothing is written.
- **Tradeoffs** — a PKM system costs ongoing maintenance effort against the value of accumulated retrievable knowledge; the effort is only justified if retrieval and creation actually happen. Light systems (a folder of files) survive with little maintenance; heavy systems (typed, linked, indexed) need a curation cadence or they rot.
- **Agent relevance** — mykb is PKM for an agent: RSIS3 captures sessions, curates concepts, and retrieves memories the same way a human would tend a slip box. The agent version automates capture and retrieval but inherits the same curation burden — the weakest step is still the one that decides quality.
- **RSIS3/mykb relevance** — this wiki is the concrete instance of PKM for the system: the standing practices (consolidation into syntheses, cross-linking, regeneration) are the human PKM loop executed by the agent, and this node documents the discipline the loop implements.
