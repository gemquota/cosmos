# Self-Explanation

## Summary

Self-Explanation — Explaining material to oneself to build deeper understanding.

## Details

- Self-explanation is generating explanations of what one is reading or solving — why a step works, what a concept implies, where it connects. Chi's studies found better learners spontaneously self-explain more, and prompting self-explanation improves learning across science, math, and programming.
- The mechanism: self-explanation fills gaps, repairs mental models, and forces the integration of new information with prior knowledge; it also exposes misconceptions that further study can fix.
- Worked example: after reading a worked math step, explain to yourself why the formula was rearranged that way and when the move would fail — this produces comprehension the step alone never taught.
- Prompting works even when the explanation is incomplete, because the effort of generation drives monitoring and revision. It underlies the explanatory half of the self-regulated-learning cycle.
- mykb relevance: L3 reflection — explaining past outcomes before planning — is self-explanation applied to agent experience.
