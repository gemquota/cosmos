# Autobiographical Memory

## Summary

Autobiographical Memory — Memory of one's own life events and their personal meaning.

## Details

- Autobiographical memory is the system of memories about one's own life — specific episodes, general events, and the life story that integrates them. It blends episodic detail with semantic self-knowledge and is reconstructive, not archival: memories are rebuilt at retrieval from fragments and schemas.
- Key phenomena: the reminiscence bump (over-representation of ages 10-30), childhood amnesia (few memories before age 3-4), and the constructive nature of recall (memories change with retelling and current self-concept).
- Worked example: recalling your first job interview is a composite — a few vivid details, general knowledge of the period, and narrative smoothing — not a video replay; two recollections of the same event can diverge.
- Autobiographical memory serves identity, social bonding, and future simulation; its distortions (source confusion, mood congruence) have forensic and clinical weight.
