# Mental Models

## Summary

Mental Models — Internal working representations of how things work, used for reasoning and prediction.

## Details

- Mental models are internal representations of systems, situations, or mechanisms that support explanation, prediction, and control. Johnson-Laird's account treats reasoning as model construction and manipulation: people reason by building models of possibilities and testing conclusions against them.
- Models are qualitative, incomplete, and often causal — a mechanic's model of an engine, a scientist's model of a reaction, a user's model of an interface. Correct models support transfer; wrong models produce systematic, predictable errors.
- Worked example: a user who believes 'deleting a file empties it from disk' behaves differently from one who models 'deleting removes the pointer' — the model drives the risk judgment and the recovery attempt.
- Learning is often model revision: instruction works by exposing, testing, and upgrading mental models (see conceptual-change). Mismatches between designer and user models cause most interface errors.
