# Chunking and Encoding

## Summary

Chunking and Encoding — Grouping information into meaningful units to fit more into working memory.

## Details

- Chunking is grouping separate items into a single meaningful unit, so that capacity limits — roughly four chunks — apply to units rather than raw elements. Miller's classic 'magical number seven' framed chunking as the workaround for short-term limits; modern estimates lower the chunk count while raising chunk size.
- Encoding converts experience into memory traces: elaboration, imagery, organization, and distinctiveness strengthen traces and create retrieval routes. Chunking is encoding's structural side — the same 16 chess pieces are one chunk for a master and sixteen for a novice.
- Worked example: the digit sequence 19452001 becomes two chunks ('1945', '2001') when tied to historical knowledge, immediately doubling what fits.
- Expertise research shows chunking is domain-specific and built from thousands of exposures, not a general memory trick. Instruction that teaches meaningful groupings (tables, hierarchies, formulas) transfers the benefit.
- mykb relevance: tagging and ontology design are chunking at knowledge-base scale — the graph's units, not raw strings, are what agents retrieve.
