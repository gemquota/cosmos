# Forgetting Curve

## Summary

Forgetting Curve — The exponential decline of memory strength over time without review.

## Details

- The forgetting curve describes how rapidly learned material fades when it is not retrieved. Ebbinghaus's original curves showed steep loss within hours and days, followed by a flattening tail; later work shows the decline is approximately exponential and highly sensitive to encoding strength and review.
- Key factors: initial learning strength (overlearning and elaboration flatten the curve), interference from similar material, and retrieval events — each successful recall resets and flattens the curve, which is exactly what spaced repetition exploits.
- Worked example: a word learned once is often lost within a week; the same word reviewed after 1, 3, and 7 days shows far better retention at 30 days than massed restudy.
- Practical use: schedules target the steep part of the curve, reviewing before the item drops below a retention threshold. Retrieval practice slows forgetting more than restudy does.
