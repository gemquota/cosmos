# Self-Regulated Learning

## Summary

Self-Regulated Learning — Learners planning, monitoring, and adapting their own learning processes.

## Details

- Self-regulated learning (SRL) is the cycle of forethought (goal setting, planning), performance (strategy use, monitoring), and self-reflection (evaluation, adaptation). Zimmerman's model frames learners as agents who set goals, select strategies, and adjust based on feedback.
- Effective SRL predicts achievement beyond ability: learners who plan, self-test, and repair misunderstandings outperform equally able peers who study passively. Strategy training plus metacognitive prompts reliably improves outcomes.
- Worked example: before a study session, a self-regulated learner sets a specific goal ('master the spacing-effect evidence'), picks retrieval practice and self-explanation as strategies, checks understanding midway, and revises the plan if a section resists.
- Barriers include overconfidence, procrastination, and weak monitoring; tools (study planners, checklists, tutors) scaffold the loop until it becomes automatic.
- mykb relevance: RSIS3's self-direction layer is self-regulated learning for an agent — set, monitor, and reflect on its own knowledge goals.
