# Hindsight Bias

## Summary

Hindsight Bias — Seeing past events as more predictable than they were.

## Details

- Hindsight bias — the 'I knew it all along' effect — is the tendency to overestimate, after an outcome is known, how predictable it was in advance. It distorts memory of one's own prior predictions and inflates the perceived obviousness of outcomes.
- Mechanisms: outcome knowledge automatically updates the causal story (creeping determinism), and memory reconstruction pulls prior beliefs toward the known outcome. It is robust across domains — politics, medicine, sports, finance — and survives expertise.
- Worked example: after a project fails, team members recall being more worried than their written records show; the decision journal, written before the outcome, exposes the bias.
- Consequences: unfair blame and credit, missed learning ('we knew it would happen' blocks post-mortem inquiry), and overconfident future prediction.
- mykb relevance: decision-journal and premortem-analysis practices exist largely to make hindsight measurable and correctable.
