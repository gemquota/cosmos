# Misinformation Effect

## Summary

Misinformation Effect — Post-event information altering or implanting memory of the event.

## Details

- The misinformation effect is the finding that exposure to misleading post-event information changes what people remember about the original event. Loftus's classic paradigm: witnesses watch an event, then receive a narrative containing a misleading detail (a 'yield' sign instead of 'stop'), and later misreport the original detail.
- Mechanisms: source confusion (post-event information misattributed to the event), updating of the original trace, and schema-driven reconstruction. The effect is strongest with plausible misinformation, delay, and repeated exposure.
- Worked example: after seeing a car accident video, participants given 'how fast was the car going when it smashed into the other car' recall greater speed and even broken glass that was never present — wording implants detail.
- Real-world weight: eyewitness interviews and media coverage can reshape memory; neutral, open-ended questioning reduces contamination.
