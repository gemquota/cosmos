# Transfer of Learning

## Summary

Transfer of Learning — Applying knowledge or skill from one context to another.

## Details

- Transfer of learning is the use of prior knowledge in new situations. It spans near transfer (same skill, slightly different context) to far transfer (structure applied to a novel domain). Classic experiments — Gick and Holyoak's radiation problem — show that spontaneous far transfer is rare unless the learner abstracts the underlying principle.
- Factors that promote transfer: multiple varied examples, practice that tests application rather than recognition, abstract representation of the principle, and learning in contexts that resemble the target. Transfer is narrow: expertise largely stays inside its domain.
- Worked example: learning the structure-mapping logic of analogy on stories about armies helps solve medical analogy problems only when the relational principle, not the surface story, is encoded.
- Modern findings temper claims of 'general thinking skills' transfer; training that transfers best is training in the target task family. Transfer is the goal that distinguishes learning from memorization.
- mykb relevance: link discipline (connecting notes by principle, not keyword) is how a knowledge graph enables transfer.
