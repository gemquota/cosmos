# Situated Cognition

## Summary

Situated Cognition — Cognition bound to context, activity, and culture rather than abstract rules.

## Details

- Situated cognition holds that knowing and thinking are inseparable from the contexts and activities in which they occur. Lave's studies of supermarket arithmetic showed shoppers computing accurately in context while failing the same math in abstract form — knowledge is co-produced with its situation.
- Implications: learning is most effective in authentic activity (apprenticeship, real problems), and abstract knowledge often stays inert when separated from use. Critics note the difficulty of defining 'situation' and the demonstrated value of some abstraction.
- Worked example: a carpenter's measurement errors and corrections make sense only against the workpiece, tools, and materials — the task's cognition lives in the interaction, not in a stored formula.
- Design: problem-based learning, cognitive apprenticeship, and communities of practice all build on situated claims; simulation and case studies import context into classrooms.
- mykb relevance: episodes and case notes keep knowledge attached to the situation it came from, countering inert abstraction.
