# Representativeness Heuristic

## Summary

Representativeness Heuristic — Judging probability by similarity to a stereotype or prototype.

## Details

- The representativeness heuristic judges the probability that something belongs to a category by how similar it looks to the category prototype — ignoring base rates, sample size, and reliability of the evidence. Kahneman & Tversky's lawyer-engineer studies showed people predict by resemblance, not by prior odds.
- Signature failures: base-rate neglect (ignoring the prior), the conjunction fallacy (Linda is a bank teller and active feminist), insensitivity to sample size, and gambler's-fallacy-like reasoning.
- Worked example: told 70% of a group are engineers and 30% lawyers, people still judge a 'quiet, math-loving' description as an engineer regardless of the base rate — similarity overrides statistics.
- Applications: diagnosis, hiring, and investing all suffer when representativeness displaces probability. Mitigation: anchor on base rates, ask what evidence is diagnostic, and convert judgments to explicit probabilities.
- mykb relevance: it is the cognitive engine behind stereotype-driven error and the reason evidence-hierarchies exist.
