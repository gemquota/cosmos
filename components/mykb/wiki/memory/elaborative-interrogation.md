# Elaborative Interrogation

## Summary

Elaborative Interrogation — Asking why questions to connect new facts with existing knowledge.

## Details

- Elaborative interrogation is a learning strategy: after reading a fact, ask 'why is this true?' and generate an explanation. The act forces the learner to connect the new fact to prior knowledge, creating elaborated, distinctive memory traces.
- Research consistently shows benefits over repetition and note-taking for fact learning, with moderate effect sizes; the effect is strongest when learners have relevant background knowledge to draw on, and it transfers to delayed tests.
- Worked example: studying 'the Sahara was once green' → ask why → construct the explanation 'orbital cycles shifted monsoons northward', linking the fact to climate dynamics you already know.
- Practical use: margin questions, self-quizzing with 'why', and reading with explanation. It is a generative strategy — a sibling of self-explanation — and pairs naturally with retrieval practice.
- mykb relevance: questioning techniques like 'why' probes are elaborative interrogation applied to notes and sources.
