# Metacognition and Monitoring

## Summary

Metacognition and Monitoring — Knowledge and regulation of one's own cognitive processes.

## Details

- Metacognition has two faces: knowledge about cognition (knowing how memory, attention, and learning work) and regulation of cognition (planning, monitoring, and evaluating one's performance). Monitoring judgments — feeling-of-knowing, judgments of learning, confidence — steer study decisions.
- A well-known failure is calibration: people overestimate what they know (Dunning-Kruger-style effects), and fluency during study is a misleading cue. Delayed self-tests and feedback improve judgment accuracy.
- Worked example: after studying, rate how well you would do on a test; then take a closed-book practice test — the gap between rating and score is your miscalibration, and the practice test is both feedback and training.
- Training metacognition — through self-questioning, planning, and post-task reflection — transfers to better learning across domains. It is the control loop of self-regulated learning.
- mykb relevance: RSIS3's pulse and reflection phases are metacognitive monitoring — observing and regulating the agent's own knowledge state.
