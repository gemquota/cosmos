# Interleaved Practice

## Summary

Interleaved Practice — Mixing different topics or problem types within a practice session.

## Details

- Interleaved practice alternates between different topics or problem types within one session, rather than blocking them. It reliably improves test performance, especially on mixed, transfer, and delayed assessments, even though it feels harder and less fluent during practice.
- Mechanisms: interleaving forces discrimination between problem types, reactivates earlier material (spacing-like benefit), and prevents the mindless application of a single just-seen procedure.
- Worked example: a math student who alternates quadratic, exponential, and logarithmic problems outperforms a student who drills each type separately — on the final mixed exam, the interleaver recognizes which method fits.
- Boundary conditions: interleaving is most beneficial for classification and discrimination learning; blocking may help initial acquisition of brand-new procedures before mixing begins.
- mykb relevance: mixing review items across domains in a flashcard queue is interleaving applied to knowledge review.
