# Concept Formation

## Summary

Concept Formation — How categories and abstract ideas are built from examples and experience.

## Details

- Concept formation is the process of constructing categories — birds, fairness, quadratic equations — from encounters with examples, features, and feedback. Accounts differ: classical (necessary and sufficient features), prototype (central tendency), exemplar (stored instances), and theory-based (causal and explanatory structure) views all capture parts of the evidence.
- Empirical findings: typicality effects (robins are more central than penguins), family resemblance, and the importance of contrasting examples in learning boundaries. Learners acquire concepts faster with varied positive examples plus near misses.
- Worked example: a child forms 'dog' not from a definition but from many dogs and the explicit contrast 'that is a wolf, not a dog' — near misses carve the boundary.
- Concept formation underlies transfer: once a relational structure is abstracted, it can be mapped to new domains. In machine learning, category learning formalizes the same problem with exemplars, prototypes, and deep feature hierarchies.
- mykb relevance: wiki tags and categories are the system's concept-formation layer — built from examples of notes, not from a priori definitions.
