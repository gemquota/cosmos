# Embodied Cognition

## Summary

Embodied Cognition — Cognition shaped by the body's morphology, sensorimotor systems, and action.

## Details

- Embodied cognition claims that thinking is grounded in bodily experience: perception, action, and the body's constraints shape concepts, memory, and reasoning. Evidence includes sensorimotor grounding of abstract concepts, gesture facilitating thought, and action-compatible effects on judgment.
- Strong versions argue cognition is constituted by body-world interaction; weaker versions hold that simulation and bodily states heavily influence, but do not constitute, thought. The debate is active, with replication of some flagship effects contested.
- Worked example: people remember action phrases better when they perform the actions; holding a warm cup biases social judgment — sensorimotor states leak into cognition.
- Applications: gesture-supported teaching, embodied interfaces, and robotics where morphology does computation. It complements situated and distributed cognition in the anti-Cartesian cluster.
- mykb relevance: if cognition is embodied, knowledge systems should store sensorimotor and procedural detail, not only propositions.
