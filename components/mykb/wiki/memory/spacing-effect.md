# Spacing Effect

## Summary

Spacing Effect — Distributed practice producing better retention than massed practice.

## Details

- The spacing effect is the finding that spreading study sessions apart in time produces better long-term retention than massing them together, even when total study time is equal. It is one of the most replicated findings in cognitive psychology, demonstrated across ages, materials, and delays.
- Key facts: longer gaps generally help retention but hurt initial performance (the study feels harder); inter-session sleep and consolidation contribute; the effect interacts with retrieval practice to multiply gains.
- Worked example: 30 minutes of vocabulary practice daily for a week beats 3.5 hours the night before the test — with the gap in performance growing as the test is delayed.
- Application: schedule reviews with expanding intervals (spaced repetition), interleave topics across sessions, and accept the discomfort of forgetting between sessions as a sign the spacing is working.
- mykb relevance: knowledge resurfacing on a spaced schedule is the same principle applied to wiki notes.
