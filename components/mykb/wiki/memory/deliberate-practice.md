# Deliberate Practice

## Summary

Deliberate Practice — Structured, goal-directed practice with immediate feedback aimed at the edge of ability.

## Details

- Deliberate practice (Ericsson) is training designed specifically to improve performance: tasks at the edge of current ability, clear goals, immediate feedback, and repeated refinement. It is distinguished from mere experience or naive repetition, which plateaus quickly.
- Key features: a coach or design specifies the task, attention is fully engaged, errors are diagnosed and corrected, and sessions are limited by concentration. Domain after domain — music, chess, sport, medicine — accumulated deliberate practice tracks expert performance better than talent or raw hours.
- Worked example: a pianist plays a difficult passage slowly with a metronome, isolates the failing measure, and alternates correction with rest — versus playing the whole piece through once for enjoyment.
- Limits: deliberate practice explains skill acquisition better than creative innovation, requires motivation and resources, and its benefits depend on task stability and feedback quality.
- mykb relevance: weekly review and gap-driven rework of notes is deliberate practice for the knowledge system itself.
