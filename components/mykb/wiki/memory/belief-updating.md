# Belief Updating

## Summary

Belief Updating — Revising beliefs when new evidence arrives, well or badly.

## Details

- Belief updating is the revision of beliefs in light of new evidence. The normative standard is Bayesian: posterior beliefs combine priors with likelihoods. Descriptive research shows systematic deviations — confirmation bias, belief persistence despite disconfirmation, and motivated reasoning that protects cherished conclusions.
- Key phenomena: belief perseverance (debunked claims keep influence), backfire effects (corrections strengthening the original belief in some cases), and the asymmetry between updating on confirming versus disconfirming evidence.
- Worked example: a trader holds that a stock will rise; after a strong earnings miss, a Bayesian updates down substantially, while a motivated trader reinterprets the miss as temporary — the two posteriors diverge.
- Improving updating: explicit priors, precommitment to update rules, calibrated confidence, and adversarial review. Systems can enforce updating discipline that individuals find hard.
