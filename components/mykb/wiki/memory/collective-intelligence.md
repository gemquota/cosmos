# Collective Intelligence

## Summary

Collective Intelligence — Group capacity to solve problems that exceeds any individual member.

## Details

- Collective intelligence is the capability of groups to think, decide, and create beyond what individuals could alone. It depends on composition, interaction, and aggregation: diverse independent judgments (the wisdom of crowds), structured deliberation, and tools that combine contributions.
- Conditions matter: independence and diversity prevent herding; aggregation rules beat unstructured discussion (Galton's ox, prediction markets); social influence can destroy or amplify accuracy; and groupthink degrades judgment without dissent mechanisms.
- Worked example: a prediction market on an election aggregates thousands of independent bets and typically beats pundits; a brainstorming session with critique suppressed does worse than the same members writing ideas independently first.
- Research (Woolley et al.) finds a general group factor predicting team performance, correlated with social sensitivity and equal participation. Digital tools scale collective intelligence to Wikipedia-scale knowledge work.
