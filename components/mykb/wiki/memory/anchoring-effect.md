# Anchoring Effect

## Summary

Anchoring Effect — Judgments biased toward an initial reference value.

## Details

- The anchoring effect is the tendency to adjust insufficiently from an initial value: final estimates remain biased toward the anchor. Tversky & Kahneman's classic study had participants estimate the percentage of African nations in the UN after spinning a wheel; estimates tracked the arbitrary number they had seen.
- Anchors work even when clearly uninformative, through both insufficient adjustment and selective activation of anchor-consistent information. Real-world effects appear in negotiation, pricing, sentencing, and forecasting.
- Worked example: a product listed at $299 makes $199 feel cheap, even when $199 is a poor price for the value — the anchor sets the comparison frame, not the product's worth.
- Countermeasures: consider-the-opposite, precommit to criteria before seeing anchors, and independent estimates from multiple people before discussion.
- mykb relevance: anchoring is why decision-hygiene processes collect independent estimates before revealing references.
