# Serial Position Effect

## Summary

Serial Position Effect — Better recall of the first and last items of a list, weaker middle.

## Details

- The serial position effect is the U-shaped pattern of recall across a list: primacy (first items recalled best) and recency (last items recalled best) with a dip in the middle. Primacy reflects extra rehearsal and transfer to long-term memory; recency reflects items still in working memory.
- The classic dissociation: a delay or distracter task between study and test eliminates recency but not primacy; increasing presentation rate weakens primacy. The effect is foundational evidence for separate short- and long-term stores.
- Worked example: after reading a 12-item list once, people reliably recall 'table' and 'window' (first and last) while 'spoon' from the middle is often lost — unless middle items get distinctive processing.
- Practical use: put the most important points first or last in a presentation or study list; mnemonic orderings counteract the dip for long lists.
- mykb relevance: flashcard queues and review sequences should expect serial-position artifacts — shuffling and spacing mitigate them.
