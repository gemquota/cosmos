# Digital Garden

## Summary
A digital garden is a personal knowledge base published as a web of linked, evolving notes — part blog, part wiki, part notebook. Notes are seeds or seedlings first, then mature through repeated revision, which values process over polish: the garden is never finished, and each note's history is part of its value.

## Details
- **Metaphor** — seeds (raw ideas) grow into seedlings (drafts) and then evergreens (stable, linked notes); nothing is finished, everything is tended. A garden with only seeds is a messy inbox; a garden with only evergreens is a blog in disguise. The lifecycle is what makes the garden a *practice* rather than a publication.
- **Contrast with blogs** — chronological, finalized posts vs growing, interlinked notes. Blogs are organized by date and frozen at publication; gardens are organized by topic and revised continuously, with dates as provenance rather than structure.
- **Concrete example** — a note "RoCE failure modes" starts as a seed with two bullets from a session, becomes a seedling with worked examples and failure modes, and finally an evergreen linked from storage, networking, and reliability clusters; each revision adds links rather than just words, so the note's position in the network grows with its content.
- **Failure modes** — gardens rot when tending stops: seeds accumulate, links go stale, and readers cannot tell living notes from abandoned ones; publishing half-baked notes can mislead readers who mistake a seed for a settled claim; and the "ever" prefix tempts authors to keep notes generic forever instead of committing to a claim.
- **Tradeoffs** — public gardens gain feedback and accountability but expose drafts and require curation effort; private wikis avoid embarrassment but lose the external pressure that keeps tending regular. The compromise is a status field that signals maturity to every reader.
- **Agent relevance** — mykb's status field (`stub` to `growing`) is a garden lifecycle: stubs are seeds awaiting cultivation by curation passes, and promotion is exactly the tending the metaphor describes.
- **RSIS3/mykb relevance** — the garden lifecycle gives the L3 consolidation loop a clear promotion rule: a stub becomes growing when it gains real substance, which is what this batch of articles performs.
