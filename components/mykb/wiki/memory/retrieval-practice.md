# Retrieval Practice

## Summary

Retrieval Practice — Actively recalling information to strengthen memory rather than restudying it.

## Details

- Retrieval practice is the act of pulling information out of memory instead of re-reading it. Decades of research show attempted retrieval produces stronger long-term retention than restudy of equal duration — the testing effect — even when retrieval fails and feedback follows.
- Mechanisms: retrieval strengthens the trace, increases future retrievability (retrieval effort), and creates desirable difficulties that improve encoding. Formats include flashcards, closed-book summaries, practice problems, and self-quizzing.
- Worked example: reading a paper once versus closing it and writing down the three key claims — the second route yields measurably better recall a week later, with feedback correcting any gaps.
- Implementation guidance: make retrieval effortful, vary formats, space the attempts, and give feedback after the attempt. Retrieval practice pairs with spacing and interleaving for the largest gains.
- mykb relevance: active recall before opening a wiki page is the same move — RSIS3's reflection phases retrieve prior outcomes before planning.
