# Mind Mapping

## Summary
Mind mapping draws a central idea with branching topics and sub-topics radiating outward, capturing associations the way a brainstorm produces them. It excels at overview and divergence — seeing the whole shape of a topic fast — and less at precise citation or cross-cutting connections, which is where labeled graphs take over.

## Details
- **Shape** — center = theme; primary branches = major aspects; secondary branches = details; colors and images aid recall. The radial layout keeps everything on one page, which is why maps work well for planning, lectures, and meeting notes where the hierarchy is clear but the details are still forming.
- **Strength** — fast, visual, and good for planning or lectures where hierarchy is clear. Creating a map forces the author to pick a center and branch structure, which surfaces missing aspects ("what branch should this go under?" is often the question that reveals a gap).
- **Limitation** — one central topic per map; cross-cutting connections need links, which is where graph notes take over. A mind map cannot cleanly express "these two branches share a mechanism" — the edge is implicit in the layout, not labeled, so the connection is easy to lose.
- **Concrete example** — mapping a reliability incident produces branches for timeline, causes, impacts, and fixes; the map gives the review team a shared overview in minutes. But the fact that "retry storms" appears under both causes and fixes is invisible in the map — it becomes a labeled edge only when the findings are promoted to wiki pages.
- **Failure modes** — branch bloat, where a map grows past a dozen branches per level and stops being an overview; unlabeled edges that hide relationships; and maps that are never translated into durable notes, so the overview evaporates with the session.
- **Tradeoffs** — mind maps are cheap, fast, and great for divergent thinking, but they are poor for long-term knowledge storage: no stable addresses, no typed relations, no versioning. Use them as a thinking and planning surface, then convert the durable content into atomic notes with real links.
- **RSIS3/mykb relevance** — a mind map is a good intermediate artifact for a planning pulse: generate the map to structure the space, then write the branches as concept pages with labeled links, which is exactly the convert-to-wiki step that keeps the map's value.
