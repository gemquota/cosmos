# Evergreen Notes

## Summary
Evergreen notes are atomic, linked, and written in your own words so they can be reused across many future contexts. They are the core unit of modern personal knowledge management and the target state of a digital garden: stable titles, precise claims, and enough connection to the rest of the network that any future project can assemble them instead of re-deriving the knowledge.

## Details
- **Properties** — one idea, own words, precise title, links to related notes, and maintenance over time. The title is a claim or noun phrase that names the idea ("Attention is a queueing problem" beats "Attention notes"); the body commits to the claim and revises as understanding improves.
- **Why** — reusable units compose: new projects assemble existing notes instead of rewriting knowledge. A synthesis of "memory consolidation practices" becomes mostly a matter of linking existing evergreen notes on spaced repetition, sleep, and retrieval practice, with a short new note for the synthesis claim itself.
- **Concrete example** — an evergreen note "Failure modes of schema migrations" gets cited by deploy, database, and reliability projects for years; because it is written as a standalone claim with its own examples, each project links to it without copying, so the note is updated once and every consumer benefits.
- **Failure modes** — titles that describe the file instead of the idea, so notes cannot be found by the question they answer; notes that quote sources instead of committing to claims, which makes them summaries rather than knowledge; and notes that are written but never linked, which defeats the network property that makes them reusable.
- **Tradeoffs** — evergreen writing costs more per note than capture (you must commit to a claim in your own words), but it compounds: mature evergreens are the difference between a searchable archive and a knowledge base. The discipline works best when combined with regular review and backlink-driven curation.
- **Agent relevance** — mykb pages with stable titles and typed links are evergreen by design; RSIS3 reuses them across pulses, so a page's evergreen quality directly determines how well the system can assemble past conclusions.
- **RSIS3/mykb relevance** — evergreens are the raw material of syntheses: the L3 consolidation practice writes distilled findings as synthesis notes that link back to the evergreen concepts they rest on.
