# Progressive Summarization

## Summary
Progressive summarization adds layers of emphasis to a note over time — bold the best lines, then highlight the best of the bold, then write a one-line summary. Each pass makes the note faster to scan and easier to resurface in future contexts, and because the layers are added only when the note proves useful, effort is never spent compressing notes that do not matter.

## Details
- **Layers** — raw text → bold key passages → highlight the best → executive summary; stop when the note serves its purpose. Layer one is capture (the raw source), layer two is the first curation pass (what mattered when you read it), layer three narrows to the load-bearing lines, and the final layer is a one-sentence claim the note can be cited by.
- **Why it works** — effort is deferred: you only compress what turns out to matter, and the compression itself is retrieval practice. Each pass re-encounters the note and forces a judgment about what is worth keeping, which is why the technique works even when no summaries are ever consumed — the passes are the learning.
- **Concrete example** — a fifty-line article capture gets its five strongest lines bolded during a first review; two weeks later a project needs one of those lines, so it is highlighted and annotated; six months later the note's summary line is enough to decide whether to open the full note — the layers let the note serve both scanning and deep reading.
- **Failure modes** — layer inflation, where so much is bolded that emphasis signals nothing; summarization without retrieval, where layers are added but never used, so the effort yields no compounding value; and the loss of context, where aggressive compression keeps the claim but drops the caveats that made it true.
- **Tradeoffs** — the technique optimizes for future scanning at the cost of a recurring review ritual; it is cheap per pass but must be repeated to work, and it suits notes that will be revisited many times, not one-off captures.
- **Agent relevance** — LLM summarization can propose layers, but the compression choices (what to keep) stay with the curator; an agent can generate candidate highlights, yet the decision of what a note means for the system's work is the part that should not be fully delegated.
- **RSIS3/mykb relevance** — summarization layers are mini-syntheses: the wiki's frontmatter description is the executive summary layer, and the synthesis notes are the final compression of a whole cluster — this node documents the layering discipline that produces both.
