# Testing Effect

## Summary

Testing Effect — The finding that retrieving information improves long-term retention more than restudying.

## Details

- The testing effect is the robust finding that taking a test on material improves later retention more than an equal amount of restudy. It holds across age groups, materials, and delay intervals, and it survives deep processing controls: retrieval itself, not just extra exposure, produces the benefit.
- Key boundary conditions: feedback improves the effect (correcting errors beats testing alone), effortful retrieval beats easy recognition, and the benefit is largest on delayed tests — the opposite pattern from cramming.
- Worked example: two groups study a chapter; one rereads it, one takes a free-recall quiz. On a test one week later, the quizzed group outperforms, and the gap grows with delay.
- Practical design: low-stakes quizzes, flashcards, and self-testing with feedback are cheap ways to harvest the effect; it underlies formative assessment and retrieval-practice pedagogies.
- mykb relevance: self-quizzing before reading a note converts passive browsing into retrieval practice for the knowledge base.
