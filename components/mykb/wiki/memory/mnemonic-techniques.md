# Mnemonic Techniques

## Summary

Mnemonic Techniques — Structured memory aids that add meaning, imagery, or order to material.

## Details

- Mnemonic techniques are deliberate encoding strategies that make material more memorable — imagery, rhymes, structure, or meaning. Families include the method of loci (spatial routes), peg systems (number-word images), keyword method (sound-similar imagery), acronyms and acrostics, and narrative methods.
- Research support: mnemonics reliably improve recall of lists, vocabulary, names, and ordered material, with effects largest when the material is arbitrary and imageable; they add little to conceptual understanding unless the content itself is meaningful.
- Worked example: memorizing the planets in order via a sentence whose first letters match — 'My Very Educated Mother Just Served Us Noodles' — converts a serial list into a cued sequence.
- Trade-offs: mnemonics take practice to build and can create an extra layer to forget; they complement, not replace, deep elaboration and spaced retrieval.
- mykb relevance: the memory layer's flashcard design can embed mnemonic scaffolds; mnemonics-research and method-of-loci entries detail the families.
