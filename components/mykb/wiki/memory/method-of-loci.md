# Method of Loci

## Summary

Method of Loci — Memory technique of placing items along a familiar spatial route.

## Details

- The method of loci, or memory palace, encodes items by placing vivid images of them at distinct stations along a familiar route — your home's rooms, a walk to work, a building's floor plan. Recall is a mental walk: each station's image cues its item, and the fixed order of the route provides serial order for free.
- Evidence: robust gains in serial recall and list learning, demonstrated in lab studies and by world-record memory athletes; neuroimaging shows the technique recruits spatial and imagery networks.
- Worked example: to memorize a shopping list of eight items, walk your apartment: bread on the doormat, milk on the coat rack, eggs on the sofa — absurd, vivid images bind item to location.
- Practice notes: routes should be well known, stations distinctive, and images interactive and bizarre; the technique generalizes to speeches and decks.
- mykb relevance: memory-palace and peg-system entries map the technique family; the wiki's spatial metaphors (navigation, graph positions) echo its structure.
