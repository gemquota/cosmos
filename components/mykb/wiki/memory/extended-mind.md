# Extended Mind

## Summary

Extended Mind — Clark and Chalmers' claim that external resources can constitute mental processes.

## Details

- The extended mind thesis (Clark & Chalmers) argues that some external resources — notebooks, phones, or a wiki — can be parts of the cognitive system, not merely instruments. If a resource is reliably coupled, serves the same function as internal memory, and is available without deliberation, it is as much a vehicle of cognition as a brain trace.
- The famous parity argument: Otto with Alzheimer's uses a notebook as his memory; Inga uses biological memory; functionally the notebook does memory work and should count as part of Otto's mind. Critics dispute where to draw the line and what 'constitution' adds beyond causation.
- Worked example: your note system, searched fluently and trusted implicitly, holds knowledge you can no longer reconstruct unaided — the system, not the head, is the remembering agent.
- Implications: cognitive offloading is not cheating but cognition; tool design is mind design; and knowledge management inherits epistemic and ethical weight.
- mykb relevance: the wiki is the clearest concrete instance — an extended mind whose maintenance is cognitive hygiene.
