# Threshold Concepts

## Summary

Threshold Concepts — Concepts whose grasp transforms understanding and opens a discipline.

## Details

- Threshold concepts (Meyer & Land) are the portals of a discipline: once grasped, they transform how a learner sees the subject. They are transformative (change perspective), irreversible (hard to unlearn), integrative (connect previously separate ideas), troublesome (resist easy learning), and often bounded (define the discipline's edge).
- Examples: 'opportunity cost' in economics, 'limit' in calculus, 'significance' in statistics, 'recursion' in programming. Learners often get 'stuck' at thresholds, oscillating in a liminal state before the concept clicks.
- Worked example: understanding that significance tests answer 'how likely under the null', not 'how likely the hypothesis', permanently reorganizes a student's reading of every study — a threshold crossed.
- Teaching implications: identify thresholds in the curriculum, design activities that confront misconceptions, and expect extended liminality rather than a single lesson.
