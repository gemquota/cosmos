# Feedback Sandwich

## Summary

The feedback sandwich is a communication technique that frames criticism between two positive statements, usually praise, then correction, then closing encouragement. It aims to make corrective feedback more palatable and to preserve the recipient's motivation and self-esteem. The technique is widely taught, though research on its effectiveness is mixed, and it can backfire when praise feels formulaic or the real message is buried.

## Details

- **Definition** — the sandwich structure is positive feedback, then the critique, then more positive or encouraging feedback.
- **Intended function** — the positive layers are meant to reduce defensiveness and keep the recipient open to the corrective message.
- **Evidence quality** — experimental studies show mixed results; recipients often detect the pattern, and sincerity of the praise determines whether it helps or annoys.
- **Alternative framing** — separating praise from critique, or giving critique as forward-looking guidance, can be more effective than formulaic sandwiches.
- **Relation to feedforward** — forward-looking feedback that focuses on next steps often outperforms criticism of the past, regardless of wrapping.
- **Worked example** — a mentor says "your report structure is strong; the analysis section needs clearer evidence; your conclusions are a good base to build on".
- **Failure modes** — invented praise, predictable patterning, and diluting the critique reduce the technique's value and can damage trust.
- **Practical relevance** — the sandwich is useful as a default structure for busy practitioners, but should be adapted to the relationship and the message.
- **Cultural and individual factors** — preferences for directness vary, so feedback framing should be calibrated to the recipient.
- **Better practices** — specific, behavior-focused, timely, and actionable feedback outperforms framing tricks alone.
