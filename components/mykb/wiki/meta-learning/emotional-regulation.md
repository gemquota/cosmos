# Emotional Regulation

## Summary
Emotional regulation is the set of processes that shape emotional experience and expression. Reappraisal (changing interpretation) and distraction are among the most effective strategies; suppression works less well and costs memory. It underpins learning, collaboration, and long-term persistence.

## Details
- **Strategy taxonomy** — Gross's process model orders strategies by when they intervene: situation selection (choosing to avoid a stressful context), situation modification (changing the context), attentional deployment (redirecting attention), cognitive change (reappraising meaning), and response modulation (regulating the outward expression, e.g., suppression).
- **Evidence** — reappraisal reduces negative affect with fewer cognitive costs than suppression: reappraisal lowers amygdala reactivity while preserving memory and social responsiveness, whereas suppression reduces outward expression but leaves physiological arousal high and impairs memory for the conversation; repeated suppression also erodes wellbeing and relationship quality.
- **When each works** — reappraisal works best on high-intensity, controllable situations where reinterpretation is plausible; distraction helps in acute moments of very high arousal; suppression is a last resort for social necessity, not a sustainable habit; and situation selection is underused — many 'willpower failures' are actually regulation failures that could have been avoided upstream.
- **Development and trainability** — regulation improves with practice and age; it is trainable in adults through cognitive reappraisal training, mindfulness practice, and repeated exposure to regulated coping, and the gains generalize to new stressors.
- **Failure modes** — overregulation suppresses useful information (anxiety about a deadline can be signal), while underregulation lets emotion hijack decisions; rumination — repeatedly rehearsing the emotion's causes — is the common maladaptive pattern that looks like processing but is actually avoidance.
- **In mykb** — failure reviews should regulate emotions first, extract lessons second: a post-mortem written in the middle of frustration captures blame, not mechanism; the after-action review process should include a cooldown step so the knowledge that gets consolidated is the durable lesson rather than the emotional reaction.
