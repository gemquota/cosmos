# Forecasting Research

## Summary

Forecasting research studies how well people estimate the probability of future events and what improves those estimates. It has found that a small minority of forecasters are consistently better than average, that calibration can be trained, and that structured practices outperform intuition. The research matters because accurate probability judgment underpins decision-making under uncertainty in business, policy, and personal life. Accuracy is measured over many judgments rather than single calls, which is why the research emphasizes calibration statistics and long-run track records.

## Details

- **Definition** — forecasting research measures the accuracy of probabilistic predictions about future events and the factors that drive accuracy.
- **Superforecasters** — a subset of forecasters outperform experts and the crowd consistently, largely through effort, numeracy, and belief updating.
- **Calibration** — good forecasters are calibrated: events they rate at 80 percent probability happen about 80 percent of the time.
- **Scoring** — accuracy is scored with proper scoring rules such as the brier score, which reward honest probabilities over confident guesses.
- **Training effects** — decomposition, frequent updating, base rates, and feedback improve forecasting performance relative to untrained intuition.
- **Cognitive practices** — top forecasters break questions into components, seek outside views, and revise estimates as evidence arrives.
- **Relation to uncertainty** — forecasting separates reducible uncertainty (answerable with effort) from irreducible randomness, directing effort appropriately.
- **Worked example** — a forecaster estimates "70 percent" for a political outcome, updates to 60 percent on new evidence, and later reviews the hit rate of their probabilistic statements.
- **Failure modes** — overconfidence, anchoring, and motivated reasoning degrade forecasts; averaging multiple independent judgments mitigates bias.
- **Practical relevance** — the findings transfer to risk literacy, decision analysis, and any setting that rewards calibrated probability estimates.
- **Team aggregation** — combining independent forecasts, especially with an explicit averaging rule, consistently improves accuracy over individual estimates.
