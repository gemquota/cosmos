# Achievement Goal Theory

## Summary

Achievement goal theory explains motivation in achievement settings through the goals people pursue, principally mastery goals focused on developing competence and performance goals focused on demonstrating it. The framework has grown to include approach and avoidance variants of each goal type. It matters because goals predict how learners approach tasks, interpret feedback, and respond to difficulty, and because classroom structures shape which goals dominate. Goal orientations are not fixed traits; they shift with the situation, so classroom climate is a lever for changing how learners engage.

## Details

- **Definition** — achievement goals are the purposes learners adopt in competence-relevant situations, guiding attention, effort, and interpretation.
- **Mastery versus performance** — mastery goals target learning and improvement; performance goals target proving ability relative to others.
- **Approach and avoidance** — each goal type splits into approach (seeking success) and avoidance (avoiding failure) forms, yielding a 2x2 framework.
- **Outcome patterns** — mastery-approach goals predict deep processing and persistence; performance-avoidance goals predict anxiety and disengagement; performance-approach effects are mixed.
- **Antecedents** — perceived classroom goal structures, ability beliefs, and achievement emotions shape which goals learners adopt.
- **Feedback interpretation** — the same grade can be informative for a mastery-oriented learner and threatening for a performance-oriented one.
- **Worked example** — two students receive a low quiz score: one asks what to study next, the other hides the score; the difference reflects their goal orientations.
- **Failure modes** — exclusive emphasis on comparison and ranking pushes learners toward performance-avoidance patterns that undermine learning.
- **Practical relevance** — teachers can promote mastery goals through task framing, evaluation practices, and messages about the purpose of learning.
- **Measurement** — goal orientations are assessed with validated self-report instruments distinguishing the four goal types.
- **Situational plasticity** — the same learner can adopt different goals in different classes, showing that structures and messages shape orientation.
