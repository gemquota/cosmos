# Problem-Based Learning

## Summary

Problem-Based Learning — Learning organized around solving authentic problems.

## Details

- Problem-based learning (PBL) organizes curriculum around authentic problems: learners encounter a problem first, identify what they need to know, study, and return to apply their learning. Originating in medical education (McMaster), it spread across professions and schools.
- Claimed mechanisms: activation of prior knowledge, relevance-driven motivation, elaboration through discussion, and transfer via contextualized practice. Evidence: PBL improves problem-solving and application; its effect on basic knowledge is mixed versus lecture.
- Worked example: a case of a patient with ambiguous symptoms launches a tutorial group — students generate hypotheses, list learning issues, research, and reconvene to revisit the case with new knowledge.
- Design challenges: tutor skill, problem quality, and coverage of foundational content; scaffolding matters to prevent floundering.
- mykb relevance: PBL is a model for episodes-driven learning — cases in the wiki become the problems that organize knowledge.
