# Mastery Learning

## Summary

Mastery Learning — Instruction where learners must reach mastery before advancing.

## Details

- Mastery learning requires learners to demonstrate mastery of each unit before moving on, with corrective instruction for those who do not. Bloom's model assumes most students can master material if given sufficient time and quality feedback — success becomes a function of opportunity, not fixed aptitude.
- Core components: clear objectives, diagnostic assessment, corrective activities for non-masters, enrichment for masters, and a second chance at assessment. Outcomes: higher average achievement and smaller variance between learners, at the cost of pacing flexibility.
- Worked example: a math course where unit 3 gates unit 4 — students who fail the unit-3 quiz get targeted reteaching and retest; the class advances as a cohort with fewer accumulating gaps.
- Evidence from meta-analyses shows moderate-to-strong positive effects, especially for basic skills; critics note time demands and the risk of shallow pacing. Mastery learning underlies competency-based education and many tutoring systems.
