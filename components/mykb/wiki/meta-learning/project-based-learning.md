# Project-Based Learning

## Summary

Project-Based Learning — Learning through sustained projects that produce authentic artifacts.

## Details

- Project-based learning (PBL) organizes learning around extended projects that produce a real artifact — a report, model, product, or performance. It combines inquiry, collaboration, and reflection, usually anchored by a driving question.
- Evidence: meta-analyses find positive effects on content knowledge, collaboration, and engagement, with quality depending on project design, feedback, and time. Key design features: authentic problem, student voice, sustained inquiry, critique, and public product.
- Worked example: a course asks teams to design a water-filtration system for a community; research, prototyping, testing, and a final demonstration make the science curriculum the means to a real end.
- Risks: group-work free riding, drifting scope, and shallow coverage; rubrics and checkpoints mitigate them.
- mykb relevance: project-based learning is the natural pedagogy for episode-driven knowledge work — projects leave artifacts worth archiving.
