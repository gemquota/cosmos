# Delay of Gratification

## Summary
Delay of gratification is the capacity to forgo an immediate reward for a larger future one, famously measured by the marshmallow test. Early correlations with later outcomes were strong but were largely mediated by family background. The construct remains central to goal pursuit and intertemporal choice.

## Details
- **Paradigm** — a child resists one marshmallow to earn two; wait time is the score. The original studies tracked children into adolescence and adulthood, reporting correlations with academic achievement, health, and income that made the task famous as a predictor of life outcomes.
- **Reanalyses** — controlling for socioeconomic variables shrinks the predictive effect substantially: family income, parental education, and household stability explain much of the association, so the task is best read as a signal of environment and skill together rather than a pure measure of innate willpower.
- **Mechanism** — success depends less on raw resistance and more on strategy: children who distract themselves, reframe the reward ('pretend it is a picture'), or move it out of sight wait much longer, showing that delay is an executive-function skill — attention control plus reappraisal — rather than a brute-force act of will.
- **Adult form** — savings, health behaviors, and long-term projects all require it: every instance of choosing a retirement contribution over a purchase, or a study session over an evening stream, is an intertemporal choice where the delayed reward is abstract and the immediate one is vivid.
- **Failure modes** — delay fails when the future reward is vague, when temptation is physically present, and when trust in the future payoff is low (the original studies also varied whether the experimenter actually delivered); environment design beats exhortation.
- **RSIS3 relevance** — self-improvement loops run on delayed rewards: a loop that only optimizes immediate metrics (token count, quick wins) will never fund the slower, higher-value work like knowledge consolidation; mykb's scheduling should budget for long-horizon tasks the same way a saver budgets for retirement.
