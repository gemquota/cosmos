# Knowledge Compilation

## Summary

Knowledge compilation is the stage of skill acquisition in which separate, consciously applied steps fuse into smooth, single procedures. It explains why performance speeds up dramatically as learners practice: the individual components stop being executed one by one and become integrated units. The concept matters because it describes the middle passage of expertise, between initial understanding and full automaticity.

## Details

- **Definition** — knowledge compilation combines small declarative steps into larger procedural units, reducing the number of separate operations in a task.
- **Relation to proceduralization** — proceduralization converts declarative knowledge into productions; compilation then composes those productions into larger, faster procedures.
- **Two subprocesses** — compilation includes composition (joining sequential steps) and proceduralization (embedding facts into rules), per ACT-R accounts.
- **Working memory relief** — compiled procedures run without holding each step in working memory, which is why experts perform complex tasks fluidly.
- **Speed gains** — the hallmark of compilation is a marked reduction in time and errors as practice accumulates on a specific procedure.
- **Specificity** — compiled knowledge is often task-specific; changing the task format can force a return to slower, step-by-step performance.
- **Worked example** — a learner who first multiplies two-digit numbers step by step later produces the answer directly after enough problems, with little conscious arithmetic.
- **Failure modes** — compilation of erroneous steps creates fluent errors that are hard to correct, and excessive compilation reduces transfer to new task variants.
- **Practical relevance** — practice design should push toward compilation through repetition, while varied problems maintain transferable skill.
- **Measurement** — research tracks response time, error reduction, and the disappearance of intermediate steps as evidence of compilation.
