# Cognitive Apprenticeship

## Summary

Cognitive Apprenticeship — Teaching by modeling, coaching, and fading expert thinking processes.

## Details

- Cognitive apprenticeship makes expert thinking visible: novices learn by watching experts model cognitive work — not just final products — then practicing with coaching while support fades. Collins, Brown & Newman's model imports the apprenticeship form into schooled domains like reading, writing, and math.
- Six methods: modeling (expert thinks aloud), coaching (feedback on attempts), scaffolding (support that fades), articulation (learner explains reasoning), reflection (comparing own process to experts'), and exploration (learner sets own problems).
- Worked example: a mentor edits an essay aloud, explaining why a paragraph moves and what the rubric really rewards; the learner then edits with prompts, then independently, then teaches another — the process, not the product, is the curriculum.
- Evidence supports gains in reasoning and transfer for complex domains; costs are time and expertise availability. Digital tutors and worked-example prompts approximate the method at scale.
