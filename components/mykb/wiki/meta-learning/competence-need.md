# Competence Need

## Summary

The competence need is the psychological need to feel effective, capable, and able to master challenges. It is one of the three basic needs in self-determination theory, and it is closely tied to self-efficacy and mastery experiences. Satisfying the competence need matters because it sustains intrinsic motivation, supports skill development, and shapes how people engage with difficulty.

## Details

- **Definition** — competence is the experience of being effective in interactions with the environment and in valued activities.
- **Basic need status** — like autonomy and relatedness, competence is a universal requirement for thriving motivation; its satisfaction fuels engagement and growth.
- **Mastery experiences** — successfully completing challenging tasks provides the strongest evidence of competence, building confidence and motivation.
- **Feedback** — clear, timely, and specific feedback lets people calibrate their competence; ambiguous or absent feedback leaves competence satisfaction uncertain.
- **Optimal challenge** — tasks slightly beyond current ability create the conditions for competence development and flow, whereas too-easy or too-hard tasks undermine it.
- **Failure modes** — negative feedback, unattainable standards, and comparison pressure frustrate the need, producing anxiety, avoidance, or giving up.
- **Worked example** — a programmer who successfully refactors a difficult module feels competent; receiving concrete praise tied to the solution strengthens that feeling.
- **Interplay with autonomy** — competence is most motivating when paired with autonomy; forced success under control does not satisfy the need as well.
- **Practical relevance** — teachers, coaches, and managers support competence by scaffolding challenges, giving process feedback, and recognizing genuine progress.
- **Measurement** — competence satisfaction is assessed with self-report scales capturing felt effectiveness in specific settings.
