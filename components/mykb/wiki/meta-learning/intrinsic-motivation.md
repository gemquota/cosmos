# Intrinsic Motivation

## Summary

Intrinsic Motivation — Engagement driven by interest and enjoyment rather than external reward.

## Details

- Intrinsic motivation is doing an activity for its own sake — for interest, enjoyment, or challenge. Self-determination theory grounds it in three basic needs: autonomy, competence, and relatedness; when those are supported, intrinsic motivation thrives; when thwarted, it withers.
- Research shows intrinsic motivation predicts deep learning, creativity, persistence, and well-being. Extrinsic rewards can undermine intrinsic motivation for interesting tasks (the overjustification effect), though they can also support it when delivered as informational, not controlling, feedback.
- Worked example: a learner who explores a topic from genuine fascination studies longer, remembers more, and transfers better than one studying to meet a deadline — even when ability is equal.
- Practical levers: choice and rationale (autonomy), challenge matched to skill (competence), and social belonging (relatedness). Curiosity-driven learning is intrinsic motivation in its purest form.
- mykb relevance: RSIS3's self-direction phase depends on intrinsic motivation to sustain open-ended improvement loops.
