# Relatedness Need

## Summary

The relatedness need is the psychological need to feel connected to others, to belong, and to be cared for. It is one of the three basic psychological needs in self-determination theory, alongside autonomy and competence. The need matters because its satisfaction predicts engagement, wellbeing, and persistence, and its frustration predicts disengagement and ill-being, especially for learners who feel marginalized.

## Details

- **Definition** — relatedness is the experience of being meaningfully connected to others and feeling that one matters to them.
- **Basic need status** — like autonomy and competence, relatedness is considered a universal nutrient for healthy motivation; its satisfaction supports intrinsic motivation.
- **Belonging and learning** — students who feel they belong engage more, seek help more readily, and persist longer, while exclusion undermines motivation.
- **Interventions** — brief belonging interventions that reframe setbacks as normal and temporary can improve achievement, particularly for groups that face stereotype threat.
- **Supportive environments** — warm teacher-student relationships, cooperative structures, and inclusive norms satisfy relatedness; cold or competitive climates frustrate it.
- **Failure modes** — social exclusion, discrimination, and anonymity frustrate the need, producing withdrawal, disidentification, and lower performance.
- **Worked example** — a study group that welcomes questions and celebrates progress meets the relatedness need, making members more willing to contribute and persist.
- **Interplay with other needs** — relatedness support often co-occurs with autonomy and competence support, and needs are more jointly satisfying than additive.
- **Practical relevance** — classrooms, teams, and online communities can be designed for relatedness through routines, peer interaction, and psychological safety.
- **Measurement** — relatedness satisfaction is measured with self-report scales that capture felt connection and care in specific contexts.
