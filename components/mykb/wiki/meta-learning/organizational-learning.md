# Organizational Learning

## Summary

Organizational Learning — How organizations acquire, retain, and apply knowledge as collective entities.

## Details

- Organizational learning is the capacity of an organization to improve through the acquisition, distribution, interpretation, and retention of knowledge. It operates through individuals but persists in routines, documents, culture, and systems — knowledge that outlives any member.
- Argyris & Schön distinguished single-loop learning (fixing errors within existing frames) from double-loop learning (questioning the frames themselves). Barriers include success traps, defensive routines, and knowledge transfer failures across teams and time.
- Worked example: a support team's post-incident review produces a runbook change (single-loop); a review that redesigns the deployment process to prevent the whole incident class is double-loop — the organization learned differently in each case.
- Enablers: psychological safety, after-action reviews, documentation, and knowledge graphs; inhibitors: blame culture, silos, and churn.
