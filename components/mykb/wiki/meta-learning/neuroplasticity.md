# Neuroplasticity

## Summary

Neuroplasticity — The brain's lifelong capacity to reorganize structure and function with experience.

## Details

- Neuroplasticity is the brain's ability to change structurally and functionally in response to experience — the biological substrate of learning and memory. It operates at multiple scales: synaptic strengthening (LTP), dendritic growth, cortical map reorganization, and system-level compensation after injury.
- Plasticity is experience-dependent and lifelong, though windows of heightened plasticity (development, post-injury, skill learning) vary by system. The same mechanisms that enable learning enable maladaptive change: addiction and chronic pain are plasticity too.
- Worked example: London taxi drivers show enlarged posterior hippocampi correlated with years of navigational experience; violinists show expanded somatosensory representation of the fingering hand — practice literally reshapes tissue.
- Practical implications: deliberate practice, sleep, and novelty promote plasticity; stress and disuse degrade it. Plasticity underlies the hope of cognitive training but also its limits — changes are specific to trained functions.
