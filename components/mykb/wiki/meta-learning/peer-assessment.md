# Peer Assessment

## Summary

Peer assessment is a practice in which learners evaluate each other's work against explicit criteria, providing feedback and often grades. It is used in classrooms and online courses to scale feedback, deepen understanding of quality standards, and develop evaluative judgment. Peer assessment matters because giving feedback can teach as much as receiving it, and because it develops the self-assessment skills learners need for independent learning. The reliability of peer assessment depends heavily on design: clear criteria, training, and calibration convert peer judgment from noise into useful signal.

## Details

- **Definition** — peer assessment involves learners judging the work of classmates using criteria, rubrics, or models, and communicating that judgment.
- **Learning through assessing** — evaluating others' work requires internalizing criteria, which transfers to self-evaluation and improves one's own performance.
- **Rubrics and calibration** — clear rubrics and training examples reduce variability and increase the reliability of peer judgments.
- **Feedback quality** — peer feedback is often faster and more frequent than instructor feedback, but varies in accuracy; calibration and anonymity affect quality.
- **Formative uses** — as a formative practice, peer assessment supports revision cycles; as summative grading, it raises reliability concerns.
- **Worked example** — students in a writing course use a rubric to score three anonymized essays, justify scores with evidence, and then revise their own drafts.
- **Failure modes** — friendship bias, harshness, free-riding, and rubric drift undermine validity without training and monitoring.
- **Practical relevance** — peer assessment scales feedback in large classes and online learning, and develops judgment valued in professional work.
- **Relation to self-assessment** — peer assessment builds the evaluative skills that underlie accurate self-assessment and self-regulated learning.
- **Design considerations** — small group sizes, iterative calibration, and weight placed on feedback quality improve outcomes.
- **Calibration practice** — having students score example work and compare with expert scores before the real assessment improves agreement and fairness.
