# Curiosity-Driven Learning

## Summary

Curiosity-Driven Learning — Learning motivated by information gaps and the drive to understand.

## Details

- Curiosity-driven learning is the self-motivated pursuit of knowledge triggered by gaps, novelty, and surprise. Information-gap theory (Loewenstein) frames curiosity as aversive tension between what is known and what is wanted — resolved by acquiring the missing information, which makes the learning intrinsically rewarding.
- Empirical work shows curiosity enhances attention, encoding, and memory: information learned during curious states is retained better, partly via dopamine-modulated hippocampal activity. Moderate gaps maximize curiosity; too little or too much reduces it.
- Worked example: a puzzle's withheld answer creates a gap; the 'aha' on resolution strengthens the association — the same dynamic powers question generation and inquiry cycles in classrooms.
- Curiosity interacts with prior knowledge (curiosity grows in domains you partly know) and with anxiety (which suppresses it). Design for curiosity: ask questions before presenting answers, use novelty, and allow exploration.
- mykb relevance: gap detection and open questions are the system-side engine of curiosity-driven acquisition.
