# Motor Learning

## Summary

Motor learning is the process of acquiring and refining movement skills through practice and experience. It produces relatively permanent changes in the capability to perform coordinated actions, from typing and driving to gymnastics and surgery. Motor learning matters because it reveals general principles of practice, feedback, and automaticity that apply to cognitive skill as well.

## Details

- **Definition** — motor learning is a set of processes associated with practice that lead to relatively permanent changes in the capability for skilled movement.
- **Distinction from performance** — temporary performance fluctuations from fatigue, motivation, or attention are not learning; learning is inferred from relatively lasting improvement.
- **Stages** — skill typically progresses through cognitive, associative, and autonomous stages, as described by the fitts-posner model, with decreasing attentional demand.
- **Practice types** — blocked, random, and variable practice affect acquisition and retention differently; varied practice often transfers better despite slower initial gains.
- **Feedback** — intrinsic feedback from the senses is supplemented by augmented feedback, whose frequency and timing critically influence learning.
- **Error-based adaptation** — the motor system uses prediction errors to update internal models of the body and environment, refining movement on subsequent attempts.
- **Automaticity** — with practice, movements become fluent and require less attention, freeing resources for higher-level strategy and decision-making.
- **Worked example** — learning to serve in tennis moves from conscious step-by-step instruction to a smooth, automatically executed action after many varied practice sessions.
- **Failure modes** — excessive blocked practice, overly frequent feedback, and avoiding difficult variations can produce temporary gains that fail to persist.
- **Practical relevance** — motor learning principles inform rehabilitation, sports coaching, musical training, and the design of practice for any physical skill.
