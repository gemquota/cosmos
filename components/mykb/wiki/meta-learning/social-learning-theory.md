# Social Learning Theory

## Summary

Social Learning Theory — Bandura's account of learning through observation, modeling, and self-efficacy.

## Details

- Bandura's social learning theory holds that people learn by observing others — modeling behavior, its consequences, and its context — without needing direct reinforcement. The Bobo doll experiments showed children imitating an adult's aggression toward a doll, with consequences modulating imitation.
- Key mechanisms: attention to the model, retention of the observed behavior, reproduction (motor and cognitive capability), and motivation (anticipated outcomes). Self-efficacy — belief in one's capability — determines whether observed skills are attempted and sustained.
- Worked example: a novice developer watches a senior refactor code: attention to the technique, retention of the moves, reproduction on their own task, and confidence from the model's success — vicarious experience building efficacy.
- Extensions: media modeling (both prosocial and harmful), self-regulation through observed standards, and mastery modeling in training. It complements reinforcement learning by adding observational channels.
