# Experiential Learning

## Summary

Experiential Learning — Learning through experience transformed by reflection.

## Details

- Experiential learning is learning by doing plus reflecting: Kolb's cycle runs concrete experience → reflective observation → abstract conceptualization → active experimentation, then repeats. Experience without reflection is just activity; reflection without experience is just theory.
- Research supports the cycle's value for skill, attitude, and transfer, while noting learners favor different cycle stages (learning styles). The model underlies labs, internships, simulations, and service learning.
- Worked example: a student leads a study group (experience), reviews what confused people (reflection), derives a better explanation strategy (conceptualization), and tests it next session (experimentation) — the cycle is the curriculum.
- Critiques: the cycle is idealized, styles are contested, and experience-based claims need evidence. Nevertheless, coupling action with structured review reliably beats passive instruction for many outcomes.
- mykb relevance: episodes and after-action reviews are the experiential-learning machinery of the knowledge system.
