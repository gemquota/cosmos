# Statistical Learning

## Summary

Statistical learning is the capacity to implicitly track regularities in sensory input, such as the frequencies and transition probabilities between stimuli. It operates across modalities and ages, often without awareness or intent. This matters because statistical learning is a foundational mechanism for language acquisition, pattern recognition, and prediction in perception and cognition.

## Details

- **Definition** — statistical learning extracts distributional information from experience, encoding which events co-occur and which follow which.
- **Implicit nature** — learning typically occurs without explicit instruction or awareness; people acquire regularities just by being exposed to structured input.
- **Transitional probabilities** — in the classic word-segmentation paradigm, learners use the probabilities of syllable transitions to discover word boundaries in a continuous stream.
- **Cross-modal generality** — statistical learning operates for auditory, visual, and tactile sequences, suggesting a general associative mechanism rather than a language-specific one.
- **Developmental role** — infants use statistical regularities to segment speech and parse visual scenes, making it an early building block of language and object knowledge.
- **Relation to sequence learning** — statistical learning over time-ordered input is closely related to sequence learning; both support prediction of what comes next.
- **Individual differences** — statistical learning ability varies across people and predicts aspects of language and reading skill.
- **Failure modes** — sparse or noisy input, competing structures, and attentional overload degrade learning; overfitting to a narrow corpus also limits generalization.
- **Worked example** — a listener exposed to a speech stream hears "ba-by", "ba-by" repeatedly and begins treating it as a unit because its internal syllable pair is highly probable.
- **Practical relevance** — the construct informs language teaching, machine learning analogies, and the design of environments that support implicit skill acquisition.
