# Inquiry-Based Learning

## Summary

Inquiry-Based Learning — Learning driven by questions, investigation, and evidence.

## Details

- Inquiry-based learning places questions at the center: learners formulate questions, investigate, interpret evidence, and communicate findings. It spans structured inquiry (teacher-chosen questions and methods) through open inquiry (learner-chosen problem and process).
- The mechanism is constructivist: knowledge is built through active investigation, with curiosity and ownership sustaining engagement. Evidence shows benefits for conceptual understanding, reasoning, and attitudes, with outcomes dependent on guidance — minimal-guidance versions can flounder.
- Worked example: a classroom asks 'why do seasons change?', designs an observation with a globe and lamp, collects data, and revises initial models — the question, not the textbook, drives the sequence.
- Balance: pure discovery underperforms guided inquiry; scaffolds (question stems, data organizers) preserve inquiry while preventing overload.
