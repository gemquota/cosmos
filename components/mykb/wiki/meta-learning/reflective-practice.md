# Reflective Practice

## Summary

Reflective Practice — Systematically examining one's own experience and actions to improve.

## Details

- Reflective practice is the disciplined review of experience — what happened, what was assumed, what worked — to improve future action. Schön framed professionals as 'reflective practitioners' who think in action and reflect on action, against the model of technical rationality.
- Models structure the cycle: Kolb (experience → reflection → conceptualization → experimentation), Gibbs (description, feelings, evaluation, analysis, conclusion, action plan), and Rolfe's 'what, so what, now what'. Reflection is most valuable when evidence-based and action-bound, not journaling for its own sake.
- Worked example: after a difficult code review, a developer reflects: what did I assume about the reviewer's goals, where did the disagreement live, and what should I change in my next review — turning one episode into a skill.
- Reflection transfers when written, linked to concepts, and revisited; unstructured navel-gazing does not. Digital tools can prompt and archive it.
- mykb relevance: RSIS3's reflection phases are institutionalized reflective practice for the agent; reflections namespace stores the output.
