# Communities of Practice

## Summary

Communities of Practice — Groups bound by shared practice who learn together over time.

## Details

- Communities of practice (Lave & Wenger) are groups of people who share a domain of interest, a set of practices, and a history of mutual engagement — a team of SREs, a research lab, an open-source project. Learning happens through participation, moving from legitimate peripheral participation toward full membership.
- Three elements: the domain (shared competence and identity), the community (relationships and mutual engagement), and the practice (shared repertoire of tools, stories, and standards). Knowledge lives in participation more than in documents.
- Worked example: a new contributor starts by fixing typos and reading issues, then review comments, then design discussions — each step is legitimate peripheral participation, and identity shifts from outsider to maintainer.
- Implications: knowledge management should support communities, not just capture content; expertise transfer rides on relationships and apprenticeship.
- mykb relevance: the wiki serves a community of practice — its conventions are the shared repertoire that membership requires.
