# Scaffolding Learning

## Summary

Scaffolding Learning — Temporary support that lets learners do more than they could alone, then fades.

## Details

- Scaffolding is structured assistance — hints, prompts, partial solutions, worked examples — that enables learners to perform just beyond their independent ability, with the support removed as competence grows. The concept extends Vygotsky's ZPD into a design principle.
- Effective scaffolds are contingent (more help when struggling, less when succeeding), fading, and transferred to the learner's own strategies. Over-scaffolding creates dependency; under-scaffolding abandons the learner to frustration.
- Worked example: solving a physics problem — first with a worked example, then with a checklist of steps, then with a single hint, then with none; each level is removed when the learner succeeds without it.
- Research shows scaffolding improves outcomes versus unassisted problem solving, especially for novices, and that fading is critical for transfer. Digital tutors implement scaffolding with hint sequences.
- mykb relevance: facilitative feedback and Socratic questioning are scaffolding moves; the wiki's growing statuses scaffold knowledge maturity.
