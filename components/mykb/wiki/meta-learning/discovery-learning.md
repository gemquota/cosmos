# Discovery Learning

## Summary

Discovery Learning — Learning by actively exploring and finding principles oneself.

## Details

- Discovery learning has learners derive principles, rules, or solutions through exploration rather than direct instruction. Bruner's version emphasized structure and intuition; later research tempered the claim — meta-analyses find unguided discovery inferior to guided instruction, while guided discovery with feedback performs well.
- The productive core: generating and testing hypotheses creates memorable, transferable understanding; the failure mode is floundering in underdetermined space.
- Worked example: given a set of examples and non-examples, a learner induces the classification rule; with a hint about which features to attend to, discovery works — without guidance, many never find the rule.
- Design: discovery works when the search space is small or scaffolds channel it; worked examples and fading outperform pure discovery for novices.
