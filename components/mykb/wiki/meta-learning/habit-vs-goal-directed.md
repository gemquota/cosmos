# Habit vs Goal-Directed Control

## Summary
Habit versus goal-directed control distinguishes automatic stimulus-response routines from flexible, outcome-sensitive deliberation. Behavior shifts from goal-directed to habitual with repetition and back when outcomes change. It explains why old habits survive intentions and why environments matter.

## Details
- **Two systems** — model-free (habit) and model-based (goal-directed) action selection: goal-directed control evaluates actions against a learned model of their consequences and flexibly re-plans; habit control stores stimulus-response associations that fire without consulting outcomes, trading flexibility for speed and low cognitive cost.
- **Outcome devaluation** — the canonical experiment: train an action for a reward, then devalue the reward (e.g., satiate or pair it with illness); goal-directed agents stop the action, habit-driven agents keep performing it for many trials. The persistence of the response after devaluation is the operational signature of a habit.
- **Shift factors** — repetition is the main driver (extended overtraining moves behavior from goal-directed to habitual), but stress, time pressure, and cognitive load also bias the balance toward habit, which is why good habits matter most exactly when deliberate control is unavailable.
- **Reversal** — habits are not permanent: when the outcome changes or the environment makes the old cue-response link fail, goal-directed control re-engages; the asymmetry is that re-engagement requires noticing the change, and environments with stable cues keep old habits alive even when the reward is gone.
- **Design implications** — make desired actions easy and visible so habits form in the right direction: implementation intentions bind a cue to a response, friction removal shrinks the goal-directed burden, and consistent context (same time, same place) accelerates habit formation; competing with an existing habit requires changing cues, not willpower.
- **mykb relevance** — the wiki and RSIS3 loops are habit-shaped too: a check-practices run is a cue-triggered routine, and its reliability depends on the cue being stable; treating the loop as a habit (fixed trigger, fixed action) is more durable than relying on motivation to run it deliberately each session.
