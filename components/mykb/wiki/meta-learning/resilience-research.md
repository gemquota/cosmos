# Resilience Research

## Summary
Resilience research examines why some people bounce back from setbacks while others languish. Findings emphasize modifiable factors: social support, cognitive reappraisal, routines, and growth framing. Resilience is a process, not a fixed trait, and it is trainable.

## Details
- **Process view** — resilience is adaptation over time, not absence of distress: momentary reactions (anxiety, anger, grief) are normal, and what distinguishes resilient trajectories is recovery speed and the eventual return of functioning, not the lack of pain; measuring a single timepoint misclassifies many people.
- **Protective factors** — the most replicated predictors are relationships (perceived and actual social support), meaning-making (narrative and purpose that reframe the setback), emotion regulation skills, routines and structure, and a sense of agency; these are environmental and behavioral, not genetic destiny, though temperament sets a baseline.
- **Mechanisms** — resilient coping works through appraisal and reappraisal: interpreting a failure as information ('what broke, and what do I control?') rather than identity ('I am a failure') changes both the emotional response and the actions taken; explanatory style — stable vs temporary, global vs specific attributions — is the lever.
- **Interventions** — CBT-style reappraisal training, post-event reflection, and structured after-action reviews improve outcomes; resilience training programs show modest but real gains, especially when they target the protective factors directly (building support networks, practicing reframing) rather than abstract positivity.
- **Failure modes** — the concept is misused when resilience becomes a demand ('just be resilient') that blames individuals for structurally adverse conditions; chronic stress erodes the same systems resilience depends on, so organizational fixes (load reduction, rest, psychological safety) outperform exhortations to cope harder.
- **In mykb** — failure reviews and after-action reviews are resilience machinery: they convert setbacks into structured learning events, but only if they run at the right time (after emotional cooldown), ask mechanism questions instead of blame questions, and record the recovery trajectory so future sessions inherit the coping pattern, not just the lesson.
