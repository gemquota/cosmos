# Posttraumatic Growth

## Summary
Posttraumatic growth refers to reported improvements in relationships, appreciation of life, personal strength, spirituality, and new possibilities after adversity. It is distinct from resilience and from mere recovery. Reported growth is real, but it coexists with ongoing distress and can be overstated.

## Details
- **Domains** — Tedeschi & Calhoun's five-factor model: improved relationships (deeper, more honest connections), increased appreciation of life, a sense of personal strength ('if I survived that, I can handle this'), new possibilities or changed life direction, and spiritual/existential development; the Posttraumatic Growth Inventory measures all five.
- **Mechanism** — growth is not caused by the event itself but by the cognitive processing it triggers: deliberate rumination — intentionally revisiting the event to rebuild a shattered worldview — plus meaning reconstruction and supportive disclosure turn a crisis into a revision of life narrative; avoidance of processing blocks growth.
- **Distinction from resilience** — resilience is bouncing back to baseline; recovery is returning to prior functioning; growth is exceeding baseline in specific domains; crucially, growth and distress are not opposites — people report growth while still suffering, and high growth often coexists with intrusive rumination and post-traumatic stress symptoms.
- **Caveats** — growth self-reports correlate with positive illusions and with personality dispositions (optimism, extraversion), so reported growth is partly perception rather than measurable change; retrospective reports inflate growth because people tell coherent stories about the past; and growth is not a required or morally better outcome — its absence is not failure.
- **Failure modes** — the concept is misused when it becomes a prescription ('you should grow from this'): pressure to find meaning can add shame to already painful experiences; in organizational settings, forcing 'lessons from failure' narratives too early suppresses the emotional processing that growth actually requires.
- **Relevance** — mykb's reflective practice can track growth narratives without forcing them: after-action reviews and episode reflections should document what changed in capability or outlook when it genuinely appears, but the wiki should treat growth notes as observations, not requirements, keeping the reflective loop honest.
