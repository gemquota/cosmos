# Self-Control Research

## Summary
Self-control research measures the ability to override impulses in favor of longer-term goals and links it to outcomes like health, grades, and finances. Trait measures predict real-world behavior better than lab tasks do. Interventions shift the environment more reliably than they strengthen the trait.

## Details
- **Measurement gap** — questionnaires (trait self-control scales) and behavioral tasks (stop-signal, delay discounting, marshmallow-type tasks) correlate only weakly with each other, and each predicts different outcomes: trait measures predict long-term life outcomes like health and financial stability, while lab tasks predict immediate task performance; researchers now treat them as separate constructs rather than one 'self-control'.
- **Trait vs situation** — situations dominate: nearby temptations, default options, and momentary depletion predict lapses better than trait scores do; the same person who scores high on trait self-control lapses when the environment makes the temptation salient, which is why situational measures (temptation exposure) add predictive power beyond personality.
- **What predicts outcomes** — when trait self-control does predict outcomes, much of the effect runs through behavior patterns rather than momentary resistance: high self-control people structure lives with fewer temptations (better routines, less exposure), so their advantage is environmental design, not heroic will.
- **Interventions** — implementation intentions (if-then plans), habit design, and friction reduction outperform willpower training: adding or removing friction changes behavior at scale, and if-then plans automate the decision point so self-control is not needed at the moment of temptation.
- **Failure modes** — ego-depletion-style training (practicing resistance to strengthen the 'muscle') shows weak and unreliable transfer; interventions that demand sustained vigilance fail in high-stress periods; and self-control narratives can become moralistic, misattributing structural lapses to personal weakness.
- **In mykb** — the self-regulation notes should distinguish trait, state, and environment effects: when a goal repeatedly fails, the wiki's post-mortem should first audit the environment (cues, friction, defaults) before blaming motivation, and capture which intervention type — plan, habit, or friction — was actually in place.
