# Theory of Mind

## Summary

Theory of Mind — Attributing mental states to others to explain and predict behavior.

## Details

- Theory of mind (ToM) is the capacity to attribute beliefs, desires, and intentions to others and to predict behavior from them. False-belief tasks — where a character believes something the child knows is false — mark its development around age four, with earlier precursors in joint attention and intention reading.
- ToM supports deception, empathy, communication, and teaching: much of pedagogy assumes the teacher can model the learner's knowledge state. Neural evidence implicates the temporoparietal junction, medial prefrontal cortex, and mirror regions.
- Worked example: Maxi puts chocolate in the blue cupboard, then his mother moves it while he is away; a child with ToM predicts Maxi will look in the blue cupboard — reasoning from his belief, not reality.
- ToM is disrupted in autism (with heterogeneity), develops atypically in some conditions, and is being probed in AI: language models pass many ToM-style tests while lacking the interactive grounding of human social cognition.
- mykb relevance: agent systems with self-models and user models are engineering ToM — predicting what another agent knows.
