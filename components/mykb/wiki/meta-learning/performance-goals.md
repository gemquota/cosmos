# Performance Goals

## Summary

Performance goals are achievement goals focused on demonstrating competence relative to others rather than on developing understanding. Learners with performance goals aim to look capable, outperform peers, or avoid looking incapable. These goals matter because they shape how learners respond to difficulty, feedback, and failure, sometimes supporting short-term achievement but often undermining deep learning and resilience.

## Details

- **Definition** — a performance goal defines success as proving ability, with others' performance as the reference point.
- **Approach and avoidance** — performance-approach goals seek to demonstrate superiority, while performance-avoidance goals seek to avoid appearing incompetent; the avoidance form is especially maladaptive.
- **Short-term effects** — performance goals can energize effort on tasks where outcomes are visible and comparison is salient, especially for capable learners.
- **Fragility under difficulty** — when tasks become hard, performance-oriented learners may disengage, hide effort, or choose easier work to protect their image.
- **Emotional costs** — social comparison elevates anxiety and ego involvement, and failure is interpreted as evidence of low ability.
- **Classroom antecedents** — normative grading, public ranking, and ability labeling push learners toward performance goals even when teachers intend otherwise.
- **Worked example** — a performance-oriented student selects the easiest presentation topic "because I can do it perfectly", trading learning for a guaranteed good showing.
- **Interaction with mastery goals** — learners often hold multiple goals; strong mastery goals buffer the negative effects of performance goals.
- **Failure modes** — over time, pure performance orientation is linked to shallow processing, cheating under pressure, and reduced interest.
- **Practical relevance** — designers of learning environments can de-emphasize comparison and reward improvement to keep performance goals from dominating.
