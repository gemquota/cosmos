# Learning Curves

## Summary

Learning Curves — Graphs of performance improvement as a function of practice.

## Details

- Learning curves plot performance (time, errors, success rate) against practice. The typical shape is negatively accelerated: large early gains, diminishing returns later. The power law of practice formalizes this — performance improves as a power function of trials — across perceptual, motor, and cognitive tasks.
- Curves are diagnostic: plateaus signal ceiling, fatigue, or a strategy dead-end; step changes signal insight or a new strategy; variability reveals instability. Comparing curves across conditions quantifies training effectiveness.
- Worked example: a typist's words-per-minute curve rises steeply in month one, then flattens; a switch to touch typing creates a temporary dip followed by a new, higher asymptote — two curves, two strategies.
- Measurement cautions: curves depend on metric choice (time vs errors), aggregation hides individual differences, and mastery of easy metrics can mask skill deficits.
