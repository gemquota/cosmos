# Personalized Learning

## Summary

Personalized Learning — Instruction tailored to individual learners' needs, pace, and interests.

## Details

- Personalized learning tailors content, pace, and pedagogy to each learner — by hand in tutoring and small classes, by algorithm in adaptive platforms. It rests on two claims: learners differ in readiness and interests, and instruction that matches those differences works better than uniform instruction.
- Evidence is mixed: well-implemented personalization (competency-based pacing, learner choice, adaptive practice) shows positive effects; superficial personalization (only pace changes) shows little. The mechanism is better targeting of ZPD and motivation, not technology per se.
- Worked example: one student accelerates through mastered algebra to geometry while another retakes targeted practice on fractions — both spend the same class period in their own zone.
- Implementation levers: formative assessment, adaptive systems, student voice, and data dashboards; risks include privacy, algorithmic lock-in, and overfitting to narrow metrics.
