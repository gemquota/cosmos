# Grit Research

## Summary
Grit research proposes that sustained passion and perseverance predict achievement beyond talent and IQ. Early claims were large; later analyses found modest effects once conscientiousness and demographics are controlled. It remains a useful folk concept and a contested scientific one.

## Details
- **Construct** — Duckworth's two-factor model: consistency of interest (sticking with one goal over years, not flitting between passions) plus perseverance of effort (working hard through setbacks); it is measured by self-report scales and, in some studies, informant reports.
- **Predictive power** — early studies reported grit predicting retention at West Point, spelling-bee finals, and teacher effectiveness beyond IQ; replications and meta-analyses find modest incremental validity over conscientiousness and mostly small effects once demographics and prior achievement are controlled, so the headline claims do not survive intact.
- **Criticism** — construct overlap with conscientiousness (grit's perseverance facet is nearly the same trait), measurement issues (self-reported grit correlates weakly with behavioural persistence), and cultural specificity (grit framing fits contexts that celebrate individual struggle; it risks blaming people in structurally constrained situations).
- **What the research does support** — sustained engagement over years predicts achievement in domains with delayed, cumulative rewards; dropping out early is the biggest cause of non-achievement; and effort compounds, so long-run persistence is real even if it is not a distinct personality trait.
- **Practical reading** — persistence matters, but domain selection and strategy matter at least as much: gritty effort in the wrong domain is sunk cost, and deliberate, feedback-rich practice converts persistence into skill. Treat grit as necessary-but-not-sufficient: a goal worth ten years of effort needs both a good bet and a feedback loop.
- **mykb relevance** — the wiki's own long-term projects (knowledge consolidation, RSIS3 improvement) are grit problems: the sustaining factor is not intensity per session but a durable reason to return, which is what the identity and purpose notes in mykb are meant to encode; consistency-of-interest metrics belong alongside output metrics in the dashboard.
