# Mastery Goals

## Summary

Mastery goals are achievement goals oriented toward developing understanding, skill, and genuine competence in a task. Learners pursuing mastery goals measure success against their own progress rather than against others. These goals matter because they consistently predict deeper processing, persistence through difficulty, intrinsic interest, and adaptive responses to failure.

## Details

- **Definition** — a mastery goal defines success as learning and improvement, so the task itself and one's own growth are the reference points.
- **Contrast with performance goals** — performance goals define success as demonstrating ability relative to others, which changes how learners approach challenge and feedback.
- **Learning outcomes** — mastery goals predict deep study strategies, preference for challenging tasks, and better conceptual understanding over time.
- **Emotional profile** — they are associated with interest, enjoyment, and lower anxiety, because failure is interpreted as information rather than as a verdict on ability.
- **Persistence** — when tasks become difficult, mastery-oriented learners maintain effort and seek help, treating setbacks as part of learning.
- **Classroom cues** — how tasks are framed, evaluated, and discussed signals which goal is valued; normative grading and public comparison can shift learners toward performance goals.
- **Worked example** — a student choosing a harder problem set "because I will learn more" is expressing a mastery goal, while choosing easier work "to look smart" is performance-oriented.
- **Variants** — mastery goals can be split into approach (striving to learn) and avoidance (avoiding not learning), with approach forms being more adaptive.
- **Failure modes** — mastery goals can become less adaptive when combined with perfectionism or when feedback is absent, leaving learners without calibration.
- **Practical relevance** — teachers and self-learners can design for mastery by emphasizing progress, offering choice, and giving feedback tied to growth.
