# Credible Commitments

## Summary
Credible commitments are promises backed by mechanisms that make breaking them costly or impossible. Credibility is what lets parties coordinate in races, treaties, and cooperation problems: a promise only changes behavior if the other side believes it will be kept.

## Details
- **Backing mechanisms** — reputation, collateral, verifiable reporting, and structural binding (making breach impossible or automatically punished) convert cheap talk into commitments.
- **Why credibility matters** — coordination problems (arms races, standards adoption, safety norms) stall when parties cannot trust each other to follow through; credible commitments are the trust scaffold.
- **For AI actors** — labs and agents gain trust only through verifiable commitment: published policies, auditable logs, and enforced checkpoints make a promise checkable rather than asserted.
- **Relationship to precommitment** — precommitment is the act of binding future behavior; commitment devices are the mechanisms; credible commitments are the resulting property others can rely on.
- **RSIS3 relevance** — the bundle's published check-practices are a credible commitment: the workspace's rules are stated and enforced, so other processes can rely on them.
- **Failure modes** — commitments that are merely asserted (no enforcement), too costly to maintain, or designed to be broken quietly undermine the very coordination they promise.

- **Verifiable reporting** — a commitment is credible when the other side can check compliance cheaply: logs, receipts, and third-party audits beat self-reports.
- **Commitment under pressure** — credibility is tested when honoring the promise is costly; mechanisms that raise the cost of breach exactly when temptation is highest are the strongest.
- **Examples in the wild** — open-source license compliance, safety-policy publication, and reproducible build artifacts are commitments whose credibility rests on checkable evidence.
- **Cost of over-commitment** — promising more than can be enforced erodes credibility; a small enforceable commitment beats a large unverifiable one.
