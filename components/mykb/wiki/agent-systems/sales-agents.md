# Sales Agents

## Summary
Sales agents qualify leads, draft outreach, and support sales conversations, personalizing communication at scale. They matter because outreach volume and personalization pull against each other, and agents resolve the tension with data-driven messaging. Compliance and consent constraints are the hard boundary on what automation may do. Sales automation's edge is relevance at scale, kept honest by consent rules.

## Details
- **Definition** — a sales agent automates parts of the sales funnel: lead qualification, personalized outreach, follow-ups, and conversation support.
- **Personalization** — agents tailor messages using prospect data, firmographics, and behavioral signals, raising relevance beyond template blasts.
- **Compliance** — consent, opt-out, and anti-spam rules constrain automation; consent-and-privacy-agents patterns govern data use.
- **Measurement** — quality is measured by response rates, meeting bookings, and conversion, not by send volume.
- **Voice** — persona-prompting and brand guidelines keep automated outreach consistent with the company's voice.
- **Worked example** — an agent scores an inbound lead against the ideal customer profile, drafts a personalized intro email, and queues a follow-up if there is no reply.
- **Failure modes** — over-automation that damages brand trust, stale prospect data, and consent violations are the critical risks.
- **Human handoff** — warm conversations escalate to humans via user-confirmation-flows and CRM integration when intent is detected.
- **Practical relevance** — sales agents show how personalization, compliance, and escalation combine in a commercially sensitive domain.
- **Opt-out handling** — unsubscribes must propagate instantly across all channels and campaigns.
- **Quality over volume** — well-targeted outreach outperforms broad blasts and protects sender reputation.
- **Worked example** — an agent scores a lead list nightly and drafts outreach only for the top tier.
- **Failure example** — a sales agent that contacts a prospect who opted out creates both a compliance and a brand problem.
