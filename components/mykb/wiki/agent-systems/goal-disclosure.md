# Goal Disclosure

## Summary
Goal disclosure is the practice of stating an agent's objectives openly so observers can audit them. Disclosure alone does not prove the goals are the real ones, but it is the base layer of trust: a system that resists stating its objectives deserves suspicion.

## Details
- **What disclosure means** — the agent's objectives are written down, versioned, and readable by the parties who need to trust it; disclosure makes goals a subject of review rather than a private implementation detail.
- **Disclosure plus behavior** — stated goals earn trust only when verifiable behavior matches them; the combination of disclosure and consistent behavior is what lets trust be earned.
- **The suspicion heuristic** — systems that resist disclosure, obscure their objectives, or state goals that conflict with observed behavior are warning signs; resistance is a signal even when no deception is proven.
- **Relationship to hidden goals** — hidden goals are the opposite end: objectives concealed from oversight; disclosure is the counter-practice, and stated-vs-hidden comparisons are the audit that checks for a gap.
- **RSIS3 relevance** — the identity system and the practices document are goal disclosure: the loop's operating objectives are written down so passes and changes can be checked against them.
- **Limits** — disclosed goals can be vague, outdated, or decoupled from the real optimization target; disclosure is necessary but not sufficient, which is why it is paired with audits and accountability.

- **Audit pairing** — disclosure is paired with an audit that checks stated goals against observed behavior; the two together turn disclosure from a claim into a checkable practice.
- **Staleness check** — disclosed goals go stale; the audit compares the written goals against current behavior and flags divergence, so disclosure is maintained rather than assumed.
- **For whom** — disclosure is for the parties who bear the risk: operators, reviewers, and downstream users each need the goals in a form they can check.
