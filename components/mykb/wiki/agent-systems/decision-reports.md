# Decision Reports

## Summary
Decision reports document context, options, criteria, and rationale for significant decisions. They enable review, audit, and learning from outcomes: a decision without a report is a black box, and an organization cannot learn from what it cannot reconstruct.

## Details
- **Contents** — the problem, the options considered, the evaluation criteria, the chosen option, the rationale, and the expected outcomes; the report records the decision at the time it was made.
- **Why they matter** — review needs something to review; audits need a trail; learning needs a record of what was expected so it can be compared with what happened.
- **Quality determines evidentiary value** — a report that omits the alternatives or reasons after the fact is a rationalization, not a record; reports are written when the decision is made.
- **Relationship to explainability** — decision reports are the organizational form of explainable decisions: they make the reasoning inspectable to reviewers and future readers.
- **RSIS3 relevance** — decision logs in the wiki make pass choices auditable: which options were considered, why one was chosen, and what outcome was expected.
- **Failure modes** — post-hoc rewriting to look good, vague criteria that cannot be checked, and reports that never get compared with actual outcomes; the last is the most common.
- **Worked example** — an infrastructure choice between two storage systems is recorded with the evaluation criteria and expected cost; six months later the report is revisited to check the prediction.

- **Revisit cadence** — reports are revisited at a set interval or on outcome milestones, and the comparison is appended; this turns reports from documentation into a learning loop.
- **Scope** — not every decision needs a report; the bar is significance and irreversibility, matching the approval-workflow trigger.
