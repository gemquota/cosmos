# Noticeboards

## Summary
Noticeboards are central pages where issues, announcements, and requests are posted for the community to see — the shared inbox of a collaborative project. They work when they are scanned regularly, kept short, and routed onward; they fail when they become dumping grounds that nobody reads.

## Details
- **Purpose** — a noticeboard makes work visible: incoming issues are announced in one place so interested parties can find them without chasing individual conversations.
- **Entry discipline** — posts should carry a subject, a one-line description, a link to details, and an owner or proposed next step; anything longer belongs on its own page.
- **Routing** — noticeboard items move through triage into tracked work (requests pages, task lists, review queues); the noticeboard is the front door, not the filing cabinet.
- **Liveness** — a noticeboard dies when posts accumulate without resolution; it needs aging rules, periodic cleanup, and a visible path from post to outcome.
- **Failure modes** — announcement fatigue (too many posts), orphan posts (no owner), and parallel boards (fragmented attention) all reduce the value of the shared inbox.
- **Relationship to queues** — noticeboards surface work; queues sequence it; the two should be connected so an announced issue becomes a tracked item automatically.
- **mykb relevance** — the wiki's curation workflow uses noticeboard-style pages as the intake surface for review queues and approval workflows.

- **Moderation** — someone must own the board: removing stale posts, merging duplicates, and nudging orphans keeps the board readable enough that scanning it stays cheap.

- **Scanning design** — entries are ordered by age or urgency, titles carry enough information to route by eye, and the board links out to details instead of containing them, so a quick scan remains possible even when the project is busy.
