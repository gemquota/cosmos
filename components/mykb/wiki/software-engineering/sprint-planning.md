# Sprint Planning

## Summary
Sprint planning turns a prioritized backlog into a committed iteration scope: the team selects items, sizes them, and agrees a goal. Its output is a plan the team owns — realistic, focused, and aligned with the product direction.

## Details
- Inputs: a refined, prioritized backlog with clear acceptance criteria and a defined team capacity.
- The team selects scope, not management; the product owner answers questions and adjusts priorities.
- A sprint goal focuses the work: one sentence on what this iteration delivers and why.
- Planning should end with a concrete plan: tasks, owners, and a definition of done for the increment.
- Capacity is evidence-based: velocity history beats gut feel about how much fits.
- Anti-pattern: planning theater where the plan is ignored by Tuesday.
- For the mykb bundle, sprint planning picks the article batch: which stubs get promoted, which clusters get new captures.

Worked example — a wiki sprint: goal 'cover the reliability cluster', scope 8 fulls and 30 stubs, capacity from last three sprints' velocity, and a check that link-verification capacity can absorb the batch.
