# Agile Methodology

## Summary
Agile is a family of approaches rooted in the Agile Manifesto: individuals and interactions over processes, working software over documentation, customer collaboration over contracts, and responding to change over following a plan. Scrum, Kanban, and XP are its most common expressions.

## Details
- The manifesto's twelve principles emphasize short feedback loops, working software as progress, and welcoming changing requirements.
- Agile is not a synonym for Scrum: it is the value system; frameworks are implementations with different ceremonies.
- Iterations deliver small increments of value, and retrospectives tune the process itself.
- The mindset costs discipline: continuous prioritization, honest estimation, and empirical adaptation.
- Anti-patterns: 'agile theater' — ceremonies without adaptation — and scaling frameworks bolted onto a culture that has no feedback loops.
- For the mykb bundle, agile habits map to small article batches, quick link verification, and weekly curation reviews.

Comparison — Scrum gives fixed-length sprints with roles; Kanban gives continuous flow with work-in-progress limits; XP gives engineering practices. All three share the agile values but optimize different bottlenecks.
