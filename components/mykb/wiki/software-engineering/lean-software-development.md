# Lean Software Development

## Summary
Lean software development adapts Toyota's manufacturing principles to software: eliminate waste, amplify learning, decide as late as possible, deliver as fast as possible, empower the team, build integrity in, and see the whole. Waste — half-done work, extra processes, handoffs, task switching — is the enemy.

## Details
- The seven wastes of software: partially done work, extra features, relearning, handoffs, delays, task switching, and defects.
- Amplify learning with short feedback loops: small experiments, prototypes, and working increments beat speculation.
- Decide late by keeping options open — defer irreversible decisions until the last responsible moment.
- Empower the team: those closest to the work make the detailed decisions; leadership sets direction and constraints.
- Build integrity in: quality is designed and tested continuously, not inspected at the end.
- See the whole: optimizing one part locally often suboptimizes the system — value streams cross team boundaries.
- For the mykb bundle, lean means deleting dead drafts, keeping capture-to-curation loops short, and pruning low-value articles.

Worked example — the wiki pipeline's waste audit found half-verified sources sitting for weeks (partially done work) and repeated format fixes (relearning). Fixing both — batch verification and templates — cut curation lead time in half.
