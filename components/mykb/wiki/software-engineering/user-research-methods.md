# User Research Methods

## Summary
User research methods gather evidence about users — interviews and observations for depth, surveys and analytics for breadth. The discipline is matching the method to the question and turning findings into product decisions.

## Details
- Qualitative methods (interviews, diary studies, field observation) explain why; quantitative (surveys, analytics, A/B) measure how much.
- Choose by the question: 'what do users need?' wants discovery; 'which design wins?' wants experiments.
- Recruit representative users; convenience samples skew findings.
- Synthesize deliberately: affinity mapping and journey maps turn transcripts into insight.
- Research is not a phase: lightweight continuous research (feedback loops, analytics reviews) beats episodic projects.
- For the mykb bundle, research covers how readers navigate and how curators work — shaping the wiki's structure.
- Worked example — the wiki team interviews five curators, finds all of them re-check sources manually, and funds a source-verification feature the interviews justified.
