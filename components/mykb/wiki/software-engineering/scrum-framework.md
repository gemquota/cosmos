# Scrum Framework

## Summary
Scrum is an agile framework organized around fixed-length sprints, three roles (product owner, scrum master, developers), and a small set of events: sprint planning, daily scrum, review, and retrospective. It turns a product backlog into working increments every sprint.

## Details
- The product backlog is a prioritized, refined list of work; sprint planning selects a subset the team commits to.
- The daily scrum is a short synchronization, not a status report to management; it unblocks and re-plans the day.
- A sprint review demonstrates the increment to stakeholders; the retrospective improves the process for the next sprint.
- Definition of done makes increments objective: code, tests, docs, and deployment steps all complete.
- Scrum assumes a dedicated, empowered team; it fails when roles are ceremonial or the backlog is commandeered by management.
- Common anti-patterns: 3-4 week sprints hiding no real iteration, and velocity used as a productivity score.
- For the mykb bundle, sprints map to weekly curation cycles with a visible article backlog and a review event.

Worked example — a sprint's plan: 12 article stubs promoted to fulls, 40 new stubs, and the link-check backlog. The review shows 52 articles landed; the retrospective decides to shrink batches after link verification slowed the sprint.
