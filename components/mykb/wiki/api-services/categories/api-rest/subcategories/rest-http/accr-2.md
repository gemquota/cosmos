## Accr 2

ACCR is an acronym encountered in the observed sessions and is associated here with accretion, the process of gradual growth by accumulation. In physics simulation, accretion describes how small particles or bodies collect into larger ones over time: dust gathers into clumps, repeated collisions and mergers produce progressively larger objects, and mass concentrates into a few dominant bodies.

In a physics engine, accretion is usually implemented as a rule rather than a force. When two objects meet the accumulation criteria, their masses are combined, their radii are updated from the new mass and density, and their velocities are merged according to momentum conservation. Energy is typically lost to heat and deformation, so the merged body moves differently than the simple sum of its parts.

Law definitions for physics engines often expose accretion as configurable parameters: a threshold mass, a stickiness factor, or a merge condition. Tuning these values changes whether the simulation produces a few large bodies or many small ones, which makes accretion a useful tool for modeling planet formation, growth of virtual ecosystems, and even cluster formation in agent populations.
