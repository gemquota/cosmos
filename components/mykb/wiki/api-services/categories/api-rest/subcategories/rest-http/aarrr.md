## Overview

AARRR is the "pirate metrics" framework for understanding the funnel of a digital product. The five stages are Acquisition, Activation, Retention, Revenue, and Referral. Each stage answers a distinct question: how users are acquired, whether they reach a meaningful first experience, whether they keep returning, whether the product generates value, and whether satisfied users bring others in. Product and growth teams use the funnel to locate the largest drop-offs and decide where to invest effort.

## Details

- Acquisition: measure installs, sign-ups, or first visits, and attribute them to channels such as ads, search, and referrals.
- Activation: the moment a user gains value — a completed profile, a first message, or a finished task.
- Retention: cohort-based return rates, which usually dominate long-term growth more than new acquisition.
- Revenue: conversion to paying plans, purchases, or other monetization events.
- Referral: sharing, invitations, and word-of-mouth loops that lower acquisition cost.

Each stage is paired with metrics and experiments. Funnel analysis identifies where users churn; activation often needs the fewest changes for the largest lift, while retention and referral compound over time. The model is descriptive rather than prescriptive — teams adapt the stages to their product type, and mobile or API-centric products may add platform-specific steps such as permission grants or onboarding completions before a user reaches activation.

## Using the Funnel

The framework is most useful when each stage has a single well-defined metric and the funnel is measured on cohorts, not just aggregates. Dashboards typically show conversion rates between adjacent stages, letting teams see whether the bottleneck is reach, onboarding, or monetization. Experiments are then scoped to one stage at a time so that improvements are attributable. AARRR is also a communication tool: it gives product, engineering, and marketing a shared vocabulary for discussing growth, and it pairs naturally with activation-oriented techniques such as onboarding checklists, retention nudges, and referral incentives.
