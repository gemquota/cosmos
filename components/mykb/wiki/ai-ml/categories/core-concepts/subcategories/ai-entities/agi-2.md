## Overview

AGI is used in the Cosmos corpus as shorthand for Artificial General Intelligence: a hypothetical machine intelligence that matches or exceeds human cognitive ability across the full range of tasks a person can perform, rather than excelling at a narrow benchmark. It stands in contrast to narrow AI, which is optimized for a single domain such as translation, image recognition, or code completion.

Discussions that reference AGI usually sit at the boundary of capability, safety, and evaluation. Because there is no agreed single test for generality, researchers propose suites of tasks spanning reasoning, planning, learning, and common-sense understanding, and they debate whether scaling current architectures or designing new ones is the more plausible route to general competence.

## Key Ideas

- Generality: one system handles many task families instead of one specialized model.
- Benchmarks: no universal measure exists, so evaluation must sample diverse held-out tasks.
- Safety: high capability raises alignment, control, and governance questions.
- Timeline uncertainty: predictions vary widely and are not a reliable planning basis.
