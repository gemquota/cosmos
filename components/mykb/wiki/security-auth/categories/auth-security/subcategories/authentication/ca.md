## Ca

CA is an acronym with two strong readings, and both are relevant to this page. The existing body records the Scala reading — Scala is a language that combines object-oriented and functional programming on the JVM — while the security context of the surrounding pages points to Certificate Authority, the trusted entity that issues and signs digital certificates. The page keeps both because the sessions do not resolve the ambiguity.

Scala's design blends both paradigms deliberately: classes and traits provide the object-oriented side, while immutable values, pattern matching, and higher-order functions provide the functional side. It runs on the JVM, so it interoperates with Java libraries, and its ecosystem includes Akka for actor-based concurrency and Scala.js for compiling to JavaScript. The language is known for expressive, concise code, with a type system that catches many errors at compile time.

A Certificate Authority is the trust anchor of public-key infrastructure. It issues certificates that bind a public key to an identity, signs them with its own key, and publishes the certificates and revocation lists that clients check. Browsers and services trust a small set of root CAs, and chains of intermediate CAs issue most real-world certificates. Compromise of a CA is catastrophic, which is why CAs are held to strict operational standards.

Both readings share a theme of trust and verification: Scala's type system verifies programs, and a CA verifies identities.
