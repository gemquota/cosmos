# Retrospective Formats

## Summary
Retrospective formats are the structures that make process improvement a habit: start-stop-continue, glad-sad-glad, the five whys, timeline reviews, and more. A good retrospective produces a few owned actions, not a wall of complaints.

## Details
- Classic format: what went well, what went wrong, what to change — followed by concrete, owned actions.
- Variants fit moods: sailboat for focus, five-whys for root cause, timeline for incident-heavy cycles.
- Psychological safety is the prerequisite: blame kills the information the format exists to collect.
- Actions must be small, specific, and owned — 'improve testing' is a wish, not an action.
- Rotate formats to fight ritual decay; the format matters less than the follow-through.
- For the mykb bundle, weekly retrospectives tune the curation workflow: batch sizes, verification speed, and linking quality.
- Worked example — the wiki team's glad-sad-glad: glad the link-check automation shipped, sad the batch was too big to verify, glad the pipeline caught it — action: halve next batch.
