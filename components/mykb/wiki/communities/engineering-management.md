# Engineering Management

## Summary
Engineering management is the discipline of leading technical teams: hiring and developing engineers, setting delivery expectations, and steering technical direction. It operates at the intersection of people leadership, project management, and architecture decisions.

## Details
- The manager's job spans three planes: the individual (growth, feedback), the team (norms, delivery), and the system (architecture, processes).
- Good management removes blockers and provides context; it does not commandeer technical details it no longer owns.
- Feedback loops are the tool: 1:1s, retrospectives, and metrics keep the team honest about progress and health.
- Managers translate business goals into technical plans and technical constraints back into business language.
- Dual career tracks (manager vs senior IC) prevent forcing great engineers into management they do not want.
- Anti-patterns: management by status update, hero-culture dependency, and unowned technical debt.
- For the mykb bundle, engineering management shows in curating the article pipeline: priorities, ownership, and cadence.

Worked example — a manager inherits a wiki platform team with unowned debt: the backlog shows 40% of capacity lost to broken link-checking. The manager funds a fix cycle, pairs a senior engineer as owner, and tracks link-health metrics monthly.
