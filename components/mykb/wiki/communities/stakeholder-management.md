# Stakeholder Management

## Summary
Stakeholder management is the practice of identifying who cares about a project, understanding their interests and influence, and engaging them deliberately. Its goal is alignment: the right people informed, consulted, and involved at the right moments.

## Details
- Map stakeholders by influence and interest: keep informed, keep satisfied, manage closely, or monitor.
- Communication is tailored, not broadcast: executives want outcomes, users want behavior, teams want details.
- Engage early and continuously: surprises are the failure mode of stakeholder management.
- Manage expectations explicitly: scope, timelines, and tradeoffs stated before they become complaints.
- Conflict is part of the job: differing interests need facilitated tradeoffs, not avoidance.
- For the mykb bundle, stakeholders include the readers of the wiki, the contributors, and the systems that consume it.
- Worked example — the wiki team maps stakeholders quarterly: readers (newsletter updates), contributors (docs and tooling), and the agent systems (API contract changes announced a cycle ahead).
