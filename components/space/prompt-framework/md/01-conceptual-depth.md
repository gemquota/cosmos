# Series 1: Conceptual Depth

**x = 3 rounds · y = 2 open-ended per round · z = 3 choices per open-ended**

Calibrates the register, audience sophistication, vocabulary, and complexity scaffolding for the entire specification. Answers feed into every subsequent series.

---

## Round 1: Domain and Audience

### Open-Ended 1.1.1
**What is the primary domain or field this prompt or project addresses? Describe its scope, core concerns, and any relevant sub-disciplines.**

Write freely. Consider the landscape, key communities, and where boundaries lie.

**After answering, choose one:**
- a) A single well-established domain (e.g., machine learning, civil engineering)
- b) An interdisciplinary space spanning 2–3 domains
- c) An emerging or niche area with evolving terminology

---

### Open-Ended 1.1.2
**Who is the intended audience for the generated output? What is their baseline familiarity with this domain?**

Write freely. Consider roles, experience levels, and what they already know.

**After answering, choose one:**
- a) Experts / researchers — people who work in this domain daily
- b) Practitioners / professionals — experienced but may need refreshers
- c) Learners / general audience — minimal assumed knowledge

---

## Round 2: Assumptions and Abstraction

### Open-Ended 1.2.1
**What foundational concepts, theorems, or prior art can the output take for granted? What must be explained from scratch?**

Write freely. List specific prerequisites by name if applicable.

**After answering, choose one:**
- a) Full prerequisites assumed — dive straight into advanced material
- b) Core fundamentals assumed; edge cases and advanced topics explained
- c) First-principles treatment — no prior knowledge assumed

---

### Open-Ended 1.2.2
**At what level of abstraction should the output operate? Should it be concrete and example-driven, or formal and general?**

Write freely. Consider the tradeoff between specificity and generality.

**After answering, choose one:**
- a) Concrete — specific examples, code snippets, case studies
- b) Mixed — conceptual frameworks illustrated with examples
- c) Formal — definitions, proofs, mathematical notation, first-order logic

---

## Round 3: Terminology and Scaffolding

### Open-Ended 1.3.1
**What vocabulary, jargon, or notation should be used or deliberately avoided? Are there established standards the output should follow?**

Write freely. Consider community conventions, style guides, or regulatory terminology.

**After answering, choose one:**
- a) Standard industry terminology — use common terms precisely
- b) Plain accessible language — minimize jargon; explain what is used
- c) Formal academic register — precise definitions, technical notation

---

### Open-Ended 1.3.2
**How should complexity be distributed across the output? Should it start simple and deepen, or maintain a consistent level throughout?**

Write freely. Consider how a reader would best absorb the material.

**After answering, choose one:**
- a) Progressive — scaffold from simple foundations to advanced topics
- b) Flat — consistent complexity level throughout
- c) Overview-first — high-level summary then independent deep dives
